# Architecture

## Layers

`client/src` has three layers with one direction of dependency, `game/ → net/ → sim/`:

- **`sim/`** — the deterministic simulation: movement, collision, interact, AI, and the seeded world (terrain, forest, clutter, trails). Pure TypeScript with no Babylon, no DOM and no wall-clock reads. ESLint forbids `sim/` importing Babylon, `net/` or `game/`, and `client/test/architecture.test.ts` names any file that does.
- **`net/`** — the versioned binary protocol, the WebRTC transport, and the host and client sessions. The host is authoritative; clients predict their own input and reconcile against host snapshots.
- **`game/`** — rendering with Babylon Lite on WebGPU: the meshes, materials and shaders, camera, HUD, audio and input sampling. It reads `sim/` state and never writes it except through input commands. Ambient wildlife placement lives here rather than in `sim/`: it is cosmetic, so peers need not agree on it byte-for-byte.

What no game owns is in `engine/`, the workspace package `@csarkosh/engine`: laid out as `client/src` is (`engine/src/{game,net,sim}`), under the same layers, and with nothing of Day Hike in it. The engine refers to nothing outside itself: ESLint refuses it an import from `client/` or `server/`, and of the package by its own name, and `client/test/architecture.test.ts` names any file of it whose import, worker entry or path leaves `engine/src`. Day Hike names an engine module by the package (`@csarkosh/engine/sim/field`), whose `exports` map a path to its file under `engine/src`; the package ships as source, so the client's build compiles the engine's modules with its own and the build is the same bytes as before the engine stood apart. A module keeps its path when it moves there, and moves when it is no game's and nothing it refers to is left behind: `tools/engine-split/move.mjs` works that set out from the list of Day Hike's own files (`tools/engine-split/dayhike.json`), moves it and rewrites every reference to it. The world's generation is there: the field, the massif, the coast (`engine/src/sim/olympic.ts`), the cliffs, the lakes' shapes, the made features, the path primitive, the vegetation, the clutter and the terrain's registry. So are the simulation's own pieces (`engine/src/sim/core.ts`: a place, a tick's input, the random stream; movement, collision, the ground, the chunk grid, the forest and its four passes) and the network's transport, peers and channels. The tick is the engine's too (`engine/src/sim/tick.ts`): every player moved from its command, on every peer, and then the game's own step on the host alone. A game hands it its rules, a record that says where a player arrives and what its record starts as, when a player is held still, what further limits a move inside the step, and what the host does once everyone has moved; Day Hike's are in `client/src/sim/world.ts` (the trailhead's start, a hiker's record, the dead held still, the wall at the road, and its rules in the order they always ran), and `client/test/sim/tick.test.ts` runs the tick under a game of a test's own. The lakes' walls and the water's level over a place are the engine's (`engine/src/sim/waters.ts`). The wire and the two sessions are the engine's as well. `engine/src/net/wire.ts` has the message types, how a number is packed, a batch of inputs and the session's own events; `engine/src/net/host.ts` and `engine/src/net/client.ts` are the host's session and a client's, which carry inputs to the host, step the world there, send snapshots back, predict the local player and blend the others. A game hands them one record (`engine/src/net/game.ts`, `NetGame`): its rules, its protocol's number, how its snapshot and its own events are packed, what a snapshot of its world holds, what a newly pressed button does on the host, how the host's word corrects the predicted world, how two snapshots are blended, and what a frame is drawn from. Day Hike's is `client/src/net/dayhikeGame.ts`, over its own snapshot and its one event (`client/src/net/protocol.ts`, whose bytes are what they were), and `client/src/net/hostSession.ts` and `clientSession.ts` make the engine's sessions over a hike's world; `client/test/net/sessions.test.ts` runs both sessions under a game of a test's own. The horror look is the engine's: the dark that closes on a heartbeat (`engine/src/game/stareLens.ts`), the figures drawn as shadow from a mask and the veil that carries them where there is no post chain (`engine/src/game/lite/shadeMask.ts`, `shadeVeil.ts`), with the grade that reads them. A game says how far each is turned; Day Hike's stare and chase do. The one renderer draws no game's parts by name. It is made with a game's side (`engine/src/game/lite/renderGame.ts`: `RenderGame`, `RenderSide`) and calls it at fixed points of making itself and of each frame, in its own order: the game's lights after its own three; the game's parts made before the scene is registered, with the mask, and after; then, a frame, its opening, the local lamp, the shot a scene takes with its lens, the lamp a scene moves, the game's own in the air, its bodies, what the view's planes hold, a path the ground cloud stands off, the dark stepped, and the haze where no chain grades. How far the horror look is turned is a record the side keeps and the renderer reads (`RenderLook`: the cast, the unsettling, the dim, the haunt, the mist come in). Day Hike's side is `client/src/game/lite/hikeParts.ts`: the hikers' bodies and the Hollows' eyes, the lamps the others wear, the cap and the scenery, the crowd, the way down's lights and haze, the scenes and endings, and the stare and the chase; its own methods (`setCrowd`, `setWay`, `setScene` and the rest) are worn by the renderer beside the engine's (`client/src/game/hikeRendererTypes.ts`, over `WorldRenderer` in `rendererTypes.ts`). The sound's bus is cut the same way (`engine/src/game/ambientAudio.ts`: `SoundGame`, `SoundSide`, `SoundHost`): it makes the world's beds and buses, and at its unlock, once every node of the world's is wired, hands a game's side the context, the shared noise, the listener's frame and the three places a game's voices may join the graph: the world's bus (muffled, hushed, limited and faded with the world), beside it (faded with the world, never muffled) and the master. A game muffles the world through the bus (`SoundHost.muffle`), and the bus wears the game's own methods beside its own. Day Hike's side is `client/src/game/hikeAudio.ts`: the stare's heart and whispers and the chase's pulse beside the world, the Hollow's call and the woods' other voices in it, the car's bus off the master, and the stare's muffle; the hike makes its bus with `createHikeAudio`. A session on a screen is two halves, and only one of them is a game's. The page's half is the engine's (`engine/src/app.ts`, `startGame`): the renderer's ladder of tiers, the governor, device loss, loading, input, the console and the pause menu, the free camera, the sessions and the loop. It names no game. It is handed one as a definition (`engine/src/game/sessionGame.ts`, `SessionGame`): the game's level, modes and action bits, where its players start, and the makers of its renderer, its sound's bus, its HUD, its sessions and its own half of a session; the game's world, state, player, renderer, bus, HUD and sessions are the definition's type parameters, and what the page needs of each is a constraint on them. Day Hike's definition is `client/src/hikeGame.ts` (`HIKE_GAME`), which the page's shell hands to `startGame`. Day Hike's half is `client/src/hike.ts` (`createHike`): its state (the acts, the way, the crowd, the woods, the inner voice, the scene, deaths and returns, the end scene, the route's trace) and the steps a frame runs for it. The page calls it at a session's own points (`SessionSide`): at rest when a session starts, a host started, a world in, a match started; before a frame's ticks, a command let through, before the renderer's sync, the game's sound, after the sync, the report; whether the match is decided, whether play is over, whether a scene holds the frame, how far the party has come; and a console command of the game's own applied. What the game's half reads of the page is one record (`SessionHost`), read afresh each time, so a renderer swapped for another is the one it sees. The host and a follower run one frame (`frame`) and one tick step (`stepTicks`). The page's shell is the engine's too (`engine/src/shell.ts`, `startShell`): the routes, the lobby and its roster, telemetry, the load check, the tier's choice and the start of a session. It is one function, which runs nothing until a game's entry calls it, and it is handed the game as a definition of its own (`engine/src/game/pageGame.ts`, `PageGame`): the game a session is of, the game's own of the page, the version of its wire, a line of status, the start of its world's worker, its staged scenes and the tier check's steps. Day Hike's is `client/src/hikePageGame.ts` (`HIKE_PAGE`), and Day Hike's entry, `client/src/main.ts`, is its two registrations and the call. Day Hike's own of the page is `client/src/hikePage.ts` (`createHikePage`): the landing page, the title's loop behind it, the intro's film over a session's start, the page for those turned away, the player's name and the mode. The shell calls it at the page's own points (`PageSide`): the landing page shown, its panel changed, a page left, Play pressed, a session's opening taken, the mute changed, a visitor turned away, the name and the mode asked; what it reads of the shell is one record (`PageHost`). The build-time values the shell reads are declared by the engine (`engine/src/env.d.ts`). A game's shipped files stay in the game's own folder and are registered with the engine by name (`engine/src/game/assetUrls.ts`, `registerAssets`; Day Hike's in `client/src/game/dayhikeAssets.ts`, which the page's entry imports), so an engine module asks for a model, a texture or a clip without knowing where it is kept. The engine's tests still stand in `client/test`, but for the empty game's. The engine carries a game of nothing, the empty game (`engine/empty/`): the coast with no place laid over it (`registerOlympic(null)`), rules that hold no one and add no step, a snapshot of the players' places, the engine's renderer side with no parts (`engine/src/game/lite/noParts.ts`) and its sound's bus with no voices (`noGameSound`), a session side that does nothing at every point, and a page side that is a title, a Play button and a line for a visitor turned away. It names the engine by the package, as a game does, and reaches outside its folder only for the engine's shipped files, which stand in `client/assets` until the engine has a repository of its own. It has its own page (`engine/empty/index.html`, `npm run dev:empty`, `npm run build:empty`) and its own tests (`engine/empty/test`, `npm run test:empty`): its world from a seed, its codec, and two peers who join, walk and see each other through the engine's sessions. It is the proof that the engine runs without Day Hike, and what a new game starts from.

The engine's coast is a terrain with nothing of a game on it, and a game lays a place over it. `registerOlympic(place)` registers the terrain `olympic` with a `CoastOverlay` or with `null`: the place's ground, staged over the cove's and under the road's corridor; the window in which the shore's climb to the massif is drawn out, and how much of the cliff bands is kept, which the coast's own stages read; its lakes; its tunables, which join the coast's and so the level id; and its answers to what the rest of the world asks of the terrain (the trail's graph and distance, the landmarks, the made features' mask, the strip where the forest comes down to the road, the clearing nothing tall grows in). Day Hike's place is the mountain (`client/src/sim/mountain.ts`: the bowl, the trailhead's pad, the peak, the lakes and the trail), and importing `client/src/sim/passes/index.ts` registers the coast with it and every world pass, Day Hike's two (the trailhead, the signs) among the engine's four. Nothing in the engine registers a terrain, so a thread that asks for a world without that import fails at once and by name; `client/src/sim/world.ts` imports it, each worker that builds a world imports it, a test that asks for a world imports it, and `client/test/architecture.test.ts` holds that the page, the admin page and every worker that reaches the terrain reach it. With no place registered the world is the plain coast and the massif behind it, and it is Day Hike's ground to the bit wherever the mountain does not reach (`client/test/sim/coastOverlay.test.ts`). Three tests hold what the move must not change: a world's identity (`client/test/sim/worldIdentity.test.ts`), a match's (`client/test/sim/matchIdentity.test.ts`) and the wire's (`client/test/net/wireIdentity.test.ts`).

Above the layers stand the page's shell (`engine/src/shell.ts`, which Day Hike's entry, `client/src/main.ts`, starts) and a hike (`engine/src/app.ts`), and neither imports Babylon, a type's included: what draws the world is `game/`'s alone. A hike holds its renderer as a `Renderer` (`game/rendererTypes.ts`: types alone, which load nothing; `createHikeRenderer` in `game/hikeRenderer.ts` makes it, and loads Babylon Lite and nothing else that draws), which it tells the hour, the weather and the view, asks to draw a frame and run its loop, and asks where its camera stands, which engine it draws with and when its world is in. An engine is held outside its renderer as an `EngineHandle` (`game/engineHandle.ts`), with nothing of Babylon in its type: a hike's renderer makes its own from the canvas the page hands it, as a staged scene's does and each step of the tier check's, and the page makes none. The scenery a hike places once (the fingerposts, the trailhead's car and board, the body at the crest) is asked of the hike's renderer in `game/hikeScenery.ts`, which loads no engine: the renderer draws it itself (`game/lite/sceneryMeshes.ts`), and a renderer with no scenery to ask gets none of it. `client/test/architecture.test.ts` names either file if it imports Babylon or reaches a renderer's scene, and names the chain if anything a hike imports, however deep, loads Babylon.js.

Inside `game/`, what is drawn is computed apart from what binds it to Babylon. For the ground, the air and the light, the numbers, typed arrays and image sources are made by modules that import no Babylon, by themselves or through anything they import, and each runs under Node with no engine: a world's float tables for the road, the trail and the made features (`terrainTables.ts`); the terrain material's uniforms by name, its own surface under the weather and what follows the tier (`terrainUniforms.ts`); the six ground layers' images and their decode (`groundLayers.ts`); the wet look's uniforms and the wet line (`wetSurface.ts`); a ring's or a patch's vertex data by attribute (`terrainVertices.ts`, over `clipmap.ts` and `facePatches.ts`); the atmosphere's and the ground cloud's uniforms for an hour and a weather (`atmosphereParams.ts`, `cloudParams.ts`, from the sky state of `skyState.ts`); the sun, the fill, the image-based light and the air's colour (`sceneLights.ts`); which mist banks stand round the camera, where the wind has carried each and how far each has faded (`mistBanks.ts`, over `mistField.ts`); the motes' settings for the hour and the weather, what their systems are made with, and the motes as a field a vertex stage can place (`motesParams.ts`, `moteField.ts`); and the camera's projection (`gameLens.ts`). The plugins, the light, the mist's and the motes' meshes and the renderer take every such value from them and hand it to Babylon Lite (`lite/terrainPlugins.ts`, `lite/wetWgsl.ts`, `lite/atmospherePlugin.ts`, `lite/worldLight.ts`, `lite/mistMeshes.ts`, `lite/motes.ts`, `lite/renderer.ts`). `client/test/architecture.test.ts` follows those modules' imports and names any that reaches an engine, and holds that no file of `src/` or `test/` imports a package under `@babylonjs/` but `@babylonjs/lite`.

A hike is drawn with Babylon Lite (`game/lite/`; WebGPU and WGSL alone). `createHikeRenderer` hands the hike `lite/renderer.ts`'s renderer, which takes its values from the modules that compute with no engine and writes them to Lite: the terrain's material and the atmosphere as material plugins in WGSL, statement for statement the game's GLSL, whose text the tests keep a record of (`client/test/fixtures/babylon/stages/`; `terrainPlugins.ts`, `wetWgsl.ts`, `atmospherePlugin.ts`), the rings and the patches as meshes written in place as the camera moves (`terrainMeshes.ts`, on textures from `groundTextures.ts`), the camera from the game's pose (`cameraPose.ts`), and the lights and the air from the hour and the weather (`worldLight.ts`).

The forest's trees are drawn the same way (`lite/forestMeshes.ts`): what each bucket is drawn with is made with no engine (the instance lists and their per-instance attributes as typed arrays and the fade bands in `forestInstances.ts`, composed with `instancePose.ts`; each plugin's uniforms as a record by name in `foliageUniforms.ts`, `distanceFade.ts`, `groundConform.ts` and `deadwoodParams.ts`; the bark's dressing in `barkParams.ts` and the trees' share of the sky in `forestLookParams.ts`), and the meshes take their values from there. The models and the bark sets are read by Lite's own glTF and KTX2 loaders (`lite/forestModels.ts`; a mesh's tangents and its material's specular weight, which the loader does not hand over, from the file's own bytes, `glbParts.ts`), each LOD's geometry is stood where its nodes put it (`modelBake.ts`), the materials are Lite's PBR with the forest's plugins in WGSL (`lite/forestPlugins.ts`: the foliage's wind and colour block, the distance dither, the base conform, the moss, the dead trees' dressing, the wet look), and a bucket's list is written in place into its mesh as the camera crosses a tree cell. Past the near radius a tree is a billboard: a quad the size of the tree's box (`impostorQuad`), turned to the camera when its list is made, whose colour and normal map are a bake, the tree's second LOD drawn side-on once into a target of its own, in the materials the near trees wear less what moves and what the weather does, as two pictures (the albedo, then the shaded normal) that are read back and made into a distance field off the page (`impostorField.ts`, `impostorFieldWorker.ts`). How a bake is posed and framed, how large it is and how long it may take are arithmetic (`impostorBake.ts`); it is drawn with a scene on a surface of its own, outside the hike's frames, and a plugin of its own in WGSL (`lite/impostorBake.ts`, `impostorBakePlugin` in `lite/forestPlugins.ts`). Seven bakes a forest, once, when its models land; nothing bakes again while it stands.

The ground cover and the cliffs are drawn the same way again (`lite/bladeMeshes.ts`, `lite/clutterMeshes.ts`, `lite/duffMeshes.ts`, `lite/cliffMeshes.ts`): what a piece is seated and tinted with is plain arithmetic (`clutterInstances.ts`, `cliffInstances.ts`), a bucket's instances are a list of typed arrays that a rebuild stages and commits and, for the blades and the grass class's cards, a cut to the view fills a drawn prefix of (`instanceLists.ts`, with `grassCull.ts`), and each field's lists are made from what its collector gathers round the eye (`bladeInstances.ts`, `clutterLists.ts`, `duffInstances.ts`, `cliffInstances.ts`); the meshes are handed those arrays. A bucket is a mesh drawn as thin instances from its list (`lite/listMeshes.ts`: the matrices the list's own array, the streams the material reads interleaved at each showing), the clutter's models come through the forest's loader, the stones are cut by `rockRelief.ts`, and the materials wear their plugins (a blade clump's collapse to its roots and a rock wall's tint toward the ground among them).

The world changes under the game's scheduler (`syncJobs.ts`): a field's crossings become jobs by one set of rules (`lite/rebuildJobs.ts`; the terrain's rings and patches by their own, `lite/terrainMeshes.ts`), a job's last slice shows its build whole, and each frame's share of them runs at the end of `sync`: 4 ms of the clock, where a builder's loop ends its slice when the scheduler's clock says 1.5 ms have gone (`sliceDue`), a job past six frames or 100 ms is late and given up to 8 ms a frame and not the frame, one a second old is run to its end, a patient job (the patches') is given a slice a frame the others leave none for, and the scheduler learns each owner's apply cost on the device and trusts it where the builder's own is less (the props' chunks are a job too, a chunk a slice). What a frame hands to the GPU is counted, and the streamed part of it held to a budget: a hand-over that does not fit what is left of the frame's waits for a frame with room, whole (`lite/frameUploads.ts`), and a ring is sent whole ahead of the frame that shows it, to the one of its two meshes that is not drawn, and a patch to a slot of the patches' one mesh that no triangle drawn names yet (`lite/terrainMeshes.ts`); in the measurement mode the last frame's record is `window.__frameUploads()`, beside `window.__syncJobs()`. What every material reads alike and what moves with the frame (the air, the wet line, the wind, the eye, the terrain's record) is one uniform block, written once a frame (`lite/frameBlock.ts`) and sent as the run of its words that moved since it was last sent (`movedWords`; nothing on a frame that moved none), and a material's own uniforms are written when it is made. The hike's own stages (the dome, the mist banks, the motes, the rain and its splashes) read the block too, for the display curve, the scene's fog, the eye and their clocks, and take the view from Lite's scene uniforms, so a frame with the camera still writes no uniform of theirs and a camera that moves writes no copy of its matrices a mesh. Every material has a named place among the opaque draws (`lite/drawOrder.ts`): Lite would otherwise draw them in the order their meshes happened to join the scene, and where two surfaces lie at one depth the later draw is the one seen.

The see-through draws follow the opaque ones and the sky, blended over the frame, tested against its depth and never written to it; Lite orders them by the depth of each mesh's origin, so each stands at the world's origin and their named places order them. The valley mist banks are the first of them (`lite/mistMeshes.ts`): every bank a frame shows is an instance of one quad, farthest first, drawn by a stage of the hike's own (unlit, the scene's distance fog, the material colour path of `lite/displayCurve.ts`), written only on a frame that moves a bank, turns the camera or changes a fade. The motes are drawn before them, on the tiers with the capacity for them (`lite/motes.ts`): every slot of the motes' capacity is an instance of one quad that the vertex stage places from its number and the particles' clock (`moteField.ts`: a life as long as a particle's, a birth anywhere in the emitter's box round the eye, the species' drift, size and fade), so nothing is kept or sent a frame but the clock, and a mote is coloured on the material colour path and added to the frame. The ground cloud is marched once a pixel by the post chain's far softening (below) on the tiers the game marches it on, in the game's steps, at the game's density for the frame (`cloudDensityNow`: the night's under the haunt and the chase's cast by how far the mist has come in, or the console's hold), over a map of the ground round the eye that is built again at once when the eye has left its middle (`cloudGroundDue`, `lite/groundTextures.ts`) and counted with the streamed world; a material's air (`lite/atmospherePlugin.ts`) marches it only where it is asked to, and then alone binds the cloud's map.

The rain is the last of the see-through draws (`lite/rain.ts`, `lite/rainSplash.ts`, `lite/rainMap.ts`). What it is drawn with is made with no engine (`rainField.ts`: the seeds, the folded time, the wind's drift, the canopy's water, the counts and each volume's uniforms by name; `splashField.ts`, the rings' the same; `rainCover.ts`: what a mesh is to the rain and its texel, the map's camera and its step rule), and the meshes take their values from there. The streaks and the drips are one mesh and one draw whose instances say which they are, the drips first, and the splashes are a second; each stage is the hike's own, a drop's seed rides in its instance's matrix, the lists go up once, and a frame writes a material's own uniforms once when the camera, the lamp or the weather has moved one of them, and nothing while it shows none: what moves with the clock is the frame's block's. The cover map is a pass of the hike's own frame with a target, a camera and a list of its own: the registered meshes (the two finest rings, the patches' one mesh, the props' boxes and the cliffs' near buckets) are drawn again from the buffers the hike's own draws read, each with a plugin that writes the height shader's texel in place of the lit colour, and only on a frame that reads the map after it has moved a step. The canopy's water is the rain's own, and the canopy over the lens is read from the forest's field (`lensCanopy.ts`), on either engine.

The hikers, the Hollows and the animals are drawn from poses made with no engine: `entityPoses.ts` steps where each hiker's body and each Hollow stands between ticks, the way it faces, its clip and rate, its eyes and aim, its lamp, and a Hollow's fades, softness, pace and reach; `wildlifeScene.ts` steps the wildlife's field, behaviour and director and says each ground member's place, turn, size and clip and each flying model's matrices, wing beats and tints, from inputs `wildlifeInputs.ts` makes of the state; `droppedCap.ts` has the cap's place, seat and shape; `characterAssets.ts`, `creatureAssets.ts` and `wingParams.ts` the files, the clips and the wings' numbers. A skinned model is baked once into a table of bone matrices, a row a frame, and every body of it is an instance with its own matrix and its own place in a clip (`lite/skinnedModels.ts`, `skinInstances.ts`): a ranger is two draws whatever the hikers who wear it and a ground species one draw whatever the herd; a body that plays on at a steady rate is written nothing a frame, since every baked mesh reads one shared clock of four bytes, sent only on a frame that shows a skinned body. `lite/entityMeshes.ts` draws the other hikers (a ranger is fetched when a hiker who wears it is first to be drawn; a capsule stands for a ranger that never came) and the Hollows' eyes, which is all of a Hollow the frame shows outside the silhouette mask: one opaque draw among the bodies', each pair's whole lit colour multiplied by its own level (a float of its instance) in linear light, before the air and the tier's curve or grade, so that the level dims what the eye gives off and what its red surface reflects alike, and the motes, the mist and the rain, which follow every opaque draw, lie over them. The lamps the other hikers wear are spot lights made for the hikers there have been at once (`lite/wornLamps.ts`): Lite builds a mesh for the lights its scene held, so they are made before the scene is registered and the scene is built again only when one more hiker than ever before joins; a hike alone lights with three lights. The dropped cap is one mesh, its crown and brim together (`lite/capMesh.ts`). `lite/wildlifeMeshes.ts` draws the ground animals as instances of their baked models and each flying model as one instanced mesh whose wing beat rides in the last row of its instance's matrix and is turned in the vertex stage (`lite/wingPlugin.ts`, the game's stage statement for statement, on a clock in the frame's block), so a flock costs its matrices a frame; the butterfly's pattern rides in its texture coordinate and its colourway in the matrix.

A lake's water is one blended surface a lake (`lite/lakeMeshes.ts`), drawn last of the see-through draws and only while a view holds its lake (`lakeSeen.ts`), with the game's shading in WGSL statement for statement (`lite/lakePlugin.ts`, `lite/waterWgsl.ts`): the bed's depth under each pixel, the ripples and the rain's rings, the one alpha it is blended by, the skin on a murky lake, and the forest's shade under the lake's skyline in place of the sky's reflection where the water is glass. What it is drawn with is made with no engine: a surface's numbers and its uniforms by name (`waterSurface.ts`), the bed's square following the camera (`bedFollow.ts`), the calm through the weather's fade (`lakeCalmFrame.ts`), the surface's grid, the skyline and the ripple bump. What every lake reads alike rides in the frame's block, so a lake in view costs its one draw and no write of its own while its calm, its shade and the bed's square stand; on the tiers with a cover map the surface stands in it as open water. A murky lake's reeds, cattails and lilies are five instanced draws placed once (`lite/waterPlantMeshes.ts`), from shapes and lists made with no engine (`waterPlantShapes.ts`), each shown while the view holds one of the 16 m cells its plants stand in. A lake's midges, dragonflies and frogs are the game's own life (`waterLifeScene.ts`, stepped with no engine for each lake within the camera's reach and handed to what draws it, `lite/waterLifeMeshes.ts`): the midges are one draw for every swarm of a lake, an instance a midge placed in the vertex stage, the game's two stages in WGSL with everything they read in one buffer written on a frame whose view holds a swarm (`midgeLook.ts`, `lite/midgeMeshes.ts`); the dragonflies a draw a kind with a unit in view, the birds' wing stage with the wing's phase, its beat and the colourway in the instance's matrix (`dragonflyShapes.ts`, `lite/dragonflyMeshes.ts`); both last of the see-through draws.

The sea is one blended draw, before the lakes' and only while a view holds water (`lite/seaMeshes.ts`), with the game's waves and shading in WGSL statement for statement (`lite/seaPlugin.ts`, `lite/seaWgsl.ts`): the swell summed at each vertex from the game's tables, the swash's sheet up the cove's face, the water's edge, the ripple bump for the wind sea on the tier with no field of it, the rain's rings, the white water, the whitecaps and the plunging lip. ## Determinism

The simulation runs at a fixed 60 Hz from a seeded RNG. The world is derived from the level id and seed alone, so every peer builds the same world locally and nothing about it crosses the wire. A test serializes two runs of the world from identical inputs and asserts they match; client prediction depends on it. The trail bench's sink and width and the litter class are part of the level id through their tunables, so a client on an older build cannot join a newer host. The two longest things a world's build opens with, the trail for the hike's seed and the pass hash (the digest of what the passes emit over a fixed window, the level id's last part), are made by a module worker started when the hike's route is entered (`client/src/game/world.worker.ts`, `worldPrep.ts`): it runs the sim's own code, the page takes each result into the sim's caches, and the build finds them made instead of stopping the page for them. The pass hash is still computed at run time in the browser that plays the hike, and one made over another count of passes is refused; with no worker, or one that fails or does not answer, the build makes both as it always did, and the world is the same to the bit either way (`client/test/sim/worldPrepared.test.ts`). The same worker then samples the hike's first scene at the tier the page decides on (`engine/src/game/preparedScene.ts`): the terrain's seven rings, the water's rings, bed and ocean field, and each lake's plants, life, surface and skyline, around the trailhead, by the functions the renderer's builders call themselves. The scene crosses with its arrays transferred, and the first renderer's builders each take their piece where it is the one they would sample for their seed, terrain, tier and start, and sample for themselves where it is not; every later renderer samples as before. The scene also carries the water's first ring geometry, its ripple bump, the made features' table and the far chorus's places on each lake's shore, and the worker's first request generates the collision chunks the first spawn reads, which the hike's chunk grid starts with (`engine/src/sim/chunkGrid.ts`). The forest has a worker of its own for the distance fields of its seven billboard bakes (`engine/src/game/impostorField.worker.ts`, `impostorFieldWorker.ts`), with the page's own computation, a bake a task, where it cannot be had.

The field math the world is derived from (`engine/src/sim/field.ts`, and the montane pipeline of `engine/src/sim/montane.ts`) also exists as a kernel in Rust (`client/rust/field`), compiled to WebAssembly and committed as `client/src/sim/kernel/fieldWasm.ts`; `client/src/sim/fieldKernel.ts` instantiates it. It keeps the simulation's rule, the arithmetic every engine rounds alike and nothing else, and returns the TypeScript's doubles to the bit, which a test asserts for every function over a spread of seeds and places, so a peer may use either. The pipeline's tunables are written into the kernel from the record the TypeScript makes for the level id, so they have one source. Nothing in the game calls the kernel yet.

The match's two acts follow the same split. The one missing hiker the poster names, the place at the crest where the body lies (`client/src/sim/search.ts`), the car at the pad, the trailhead board at the trail's entrance, turned to face where a player arrives, with the poster on its face (`client/src/sim/trailhead.ts`), where a player arrives and the way they face — in front of the car, toward the trail's entrance (`client/src/sim/spawn.ts`) — the sign posts at the trail's forks, one plank per place, each place once on a post on the arm with the shortest trail to it and the Summit on top, (`client/src/sim/signs.ts`), with every pond and meadow named from a seeded stream of its own so naming moves no other draw (`client/src/sim/placeNames.ts`), and the wall at the road's edge (`client/src/sim/containment.ts`) are all derived from the seed and the trail graph on every peer. The rules over them are host state (`client/src/sim/summit.ts`): the phase — a climb until the first living player comes within 12 m of the body, a chase from that tick on — whether each player stands on the road corridor, which is safe ground a Hollow never enters, and the match's outcome, which settles the tick the first player who was out on the mountain in the chase is back on the road (nothing kills from then on). The phase, each player's safe bit and the outcome ride every snapshot, with why each player last died (protocol 6), and the wall clamps inside the movement step so a client predicts it exactly. That graph is built by `client/src/sim/trailBuild.ts` on the shared routing plumbing in `client/src/sim/trailPlan.ts` — a stem from the pad to the crest with its seeded loops, and below the crest a web of parallel strands cross-linked by rungs (`client/src/sim/trailBraid.ts`) — and carries its junctions and every node's trail distance home as `forks` and `homeDist`.

At the trailhead the forest comes down to the road, and the trail leaves the pad inland. The shore's rules — where trees thin toward the beach, where grass, rocks, fungi and bushes stop, where the ground is painted sand, and the ground the forest's animals keep to — read the ground's height, and at the trailhead they read it through a strip (`client/src/sim/shoreStrip.ts`): a weight that is a function of a place in the road's frame alone, 1 within 30 m of the pad along the road and fading over the next 15 m, from the road's bed inland to 100 m and fading over the next 20 m, and 0 at the road's centreline and seaward of it. `shoreHeight` is the ground's height plus 9 m times the weight, so inside the strip the rules read forest ground; the forest's density is at least the weight itself there, times the road's own gate. The terrain's own heights are not changed: the strip moves what grows and what is painted, never the ground's shape, and the engine reads it through the terrain's `shoreStrip` hook, which the mountain answers with it: `engine/src/sim/vegetation.ts`, `engine/src/sim/clutter.ts`, `engine/src/game/terrainSurface.ts` and `engine/src/game/wildlifeField.ts`. The trail's search keeps off the shore (`closeShore` in `client/src/sim/trailGrid.ts`, called by `client/src/sim/trailBuild.ts`): every cell of its grid whose ground is under 9 m or that lies within 30 m of the road's centreline is closed, by the ground's own height and not the strip's, but for the two rows of cells straight inland of the pad, the doorway. It closes cells and opens none, so a doorway cell too steep for the search stays closed. Round the pad is the trailhead's clearing (`TRAILHEAD_CLEARING` in `engine/src/sim/clutter.ts`): within 24 m of the pad's centre no rock, boulder, fungus or bush stands, so where a player arrives, the entrance and the board are in the open. The board is judged by the way a player faces as they arrive (`facingDir` in `engine/src/sim/facing.ts`, sine and cosine as polynomials, since `sim/` uses no trigonometry of the platform's). The strip's numbers, the search's and the clearing's are part of the level id through their tunables.

The water's ground is the sim's too. The trail's pond is a valley lake (`engine/src/sim/lakeShape.ts`, `docs/terrain/2026-10-10-lakes-and-massif-design.md`): its shore is where a signed shore distance s, positive in the water, crosses zero, s read with exact derivatives by `lakeShoreInto` from a basin field about a bent spine of two to four points, drawn longer than it is wide, with harmonics faded in away from its centre, a fine wobble, bumps for points and an outlet's spout. Its murk follows where it lies on the climb from the pad to the crest. Its ground (`lakeGroundD`, `features.ts`) is laid as the bowl is built, at the place the build chose for it, so every trail and prop sees it: a shore shelf and a drop to a middle as deep as its water is clear, a bank from the water's edge steep on its steep side and gentle on the other, an apron easing into the ground about it, and at the outlet a notch to a sill 0.3 m over the level, so the water could only leave there; no trail runs within 7 m of its shore (`trailBuild.ts`). A lake's loop walks a band 12.7 to 19 m outside its shore and the braid keeps 15 m off it; its level is the mean ground inside its outline, raised by up to 1 m where its spout needs it to spill; these numbers and every constant of the shore's draw are tunables, so the level id moved with the lakes. On a murky lake a marsh lies on the shelf at one end, in the shore's own coordinates, its ground held at the water's level and applied to the composed field after the bowl is built, and a wall at the shelf's edge, found along the shore distance's slope, keeps a wading player's camera above the water (`containment.ts`). The shore in front of the pad is a pebble cove between two headlands, seaward of the road's corridor only (`olympic.ts`). The variant lists its water (`waterBodies`: the sea, then each lake with its shape and the bound past which its ground is untouched) and the cove's weight (`coveMask`) for the renderer, the clutter, the ground paint and the wading; a lake has no radius, and every reader of its extent asks its shape.

The Hollow (`client/src/sim/hollow.ts`) is one `EnemyState` with four `AiState` values — Emerge, Hunt, Stand and Watch, the last being the climb's watcher (`client/src/sim/watcher.ts`), which stands off the trail facing the lead and is spawned when it shows and removed when it hides; off since 2026-10-05 (`WATCHER_ON_CLIMB`). The night's figures are the haunt's (`client/src/sim/haunt.ts`): a host-only director stands shades up at the edge of a player's sight once the night is in and through the chase, as enemies in the snapshot with two more `AiState` values, Shade (harmless, gone when neared, watched or timed out) and Lunge (walks its line at a player and kills on contact); in the chase they stand off the way down. Each screen adds its own slight vignette and whispers (`client/src/game/escalation.ts`), and the views fade them in and out (`client/src/game/entityPoses.ts`) — so the snapshot's existing enemy channel carries it unchanged, no new wire shape needed. Its rules and the routes it walks (`engine/src/sim/trailRoute.ts`) run host-only, inside the same authoritative tick as everything else in `sim/`. The way down (`client/src/sim/wayDown.ts`, `wayDraw.ts`; `docs/gameplay/2026-11-13-the-way-down.md`) is the one path the chase holds a player to, from the crest to the pad: `World.way`, on `World` rather than `WorldState` and never on the wire, because every peer draws the same one from the seed alone, a slice a frame from early in the climb, with arithmetic every browser rounds alike; the chase's rules read it through `client/src/sim/chaseLine.ts`, and each screen draws it in mist (`client/src/game/wayLight.ts`, `client/src/game/lite/wayLightMeshes.ts`). The crowd of shades beside it in the chase is not the sim's at all (`client/src/game/crowdField.ts`): each screen makes, moves and draws up to ninety of its own round its own player from its own random stream (`client/src/game/lite/crowdEyeMeshes.ts`, `crowdCardMeshes.ts`), never on the wire, and gives the nearest one up to a killer the host raises beside it. None of them comes the last metres to a player, but a player who walks into one dies of it: only their own screen can know, so it says so in a bit of its commands' buttons (`Button.Taken`) and the host makes the death (`client/src/sim/taken.ts`), taking a player's word for their own death and nothing else. (Until 2026-11-13 the chase drew a hidden guide route from the world RNG and stood a Hollow in every branch the guide did not take.) The watcher's record is host-only state of the same kind (`World.watcher`: whether it is shown, the rest until it shows again, and its own random stream, so the climb never moves the world's); peers only ever receive more Hollows. Each player's stare at it rides the snapshot as one byte, and kills nobody: the player's own screen and ears take it (`engine/src/game/stareLens.ts`, `stareAudio.ts`) as a darkness that closes from the Hollow's side on a heartbeat, a world gone muffled, and whispers. The climb is three acts by the party's progress (`client/src/game/escalation.ts`): a semi-sunny day, then cloud and rain, then night. It has a voice of its own (`client/src/game/woodsVoice.ts`, `woodsSounds.ts`): a birdsong bed that halves in the wet and is gone by night, when sounds that are not animals take its place, and the Hollow's call from up the trail at six marks of the night, nearer each time. Death, on every level, is permanent: there is no respawn. A death in a forest match's chase is not permanent (2026-11-13): the host brings the player back on the crest six seconds on, one Hollow moved there to meet them and nothing killing them for four seconds, records why they died for the line they say, and the match is won when the first player who was out on the mountain reaches the road (`client/src/sim/checkpoint.ts`, `returns.ts`, `deathCause.ts`; [the rules](docs/gameplay/2026-09-16-the-summit.md)).

## Rendering

The world streams around the camera in rings: full geometry near, cheaper levels of detail further out, and a far field beyond that is kept full to the horizon (2026-10-15, [its design](docs/rendering/2026-10-15-far-field-fullness-design.md)): the ground past the detail's fade carries drifts at 10 m and 30 m and a macro tint at 60 m and 220 m and goes matte with distance (`groundHexParams.ts`, `lite/terrainPlugins.ts`); the ground cover thins by a ramp, each instance to its own distance, rather than ending at a line, and the meadow's cards go on past 40 m to 100 m with one in two kept and widened to twice its width (`clutterField.ts`, `distanceFade.ts`); each clipmap ring blends its colour and gradient toward the coarser ring's as it blends the height (`clipmap.ts`); the far trees are cards of two unlit bakes a cohort, the species' colour and its normal map, read back and passed through a signed distance field so the rim stays a curve at any size, lit once live, the stride lattice's cards grown past a kilometre (`lite/forestMeshes.ts`, `lite/impostorBake.ts`, `impostorField.ts`). The sun casts no shadow on any tier since 2026-10-28: the map cost a fifth to a third of a medium frame on the Air ([the measurement](docs/rendering/2026-10-27-shadow-cascade-flicker.md#the-shadow-map-dropped-2026-10-28)); the high tier drew three cascades to 1500 m before, medium one to 300 m. What a step over a cell line changes — the terrain's rings, the ground cover's lists, the forest's bands — is rebuilt as a job run in slices over the frames that follow, at most 4 ms of them a frame, its buffers uploaded together in its last slice and never more than six frames or 100 ms after the crossing, whichever comes first (`engine/src/game/syncJobs.ts`); the first build, a teleport and the free camera's boost rebuild at once. A terrain ring keeps each vertex's lifted height and normal, so a step samples only the strip it moves onto, which, with the budget jobs leave, it samples ahead onto the line the camera is heading for (`clipmap.ts`); the ground cover's collectors keep their squares of cells and look up only the strip a step adds, which they too look up ahead, as the forest collects ahead the bands of the next cell it is heading into (`cellWindow.ts`). Quality tiers (`low`, `medium`, `high`, `engine/src/game/quality.ts`) set hardware scaling, the shadow map's size, cascades and reach (none on every tier), and the forest's and cover's radii (the tier's `lodBias` and `textureMipCap` are read by nothing); the tier is chosen from the GPU the browser names (`gpuSignals.ts`, `gpuClass.ts`), confirmed by a short measurement of the heaviest standard view where the browser will not name it (`frameProbe.ts`), and can be forced with `?tier=low|medium|high`. The player can set the tier in Settings, on the title screen and the pause screen (`settings.ts`); Auto is the default. Settings also holds the mode (`client/src/game/gameMode.ts`, the `/mode` command): `game`, the hike as it is; `explore`, the same mountain with no one in it and nothing to dread, where the sim builds no watcher and no haunt and the summit is never found, the wall at the road comes down so the road and the beach beyond it are open, the escalation's sky, lens and chase are held at nothing, the woods' calls and the night's sounds never come, the inner voice keeps only the trailhead's line and its answers to the weather, and the day is the explore day's (`exploreDay.ts`): a clock round the whole day at forty seconds an hour, its nights and its mists among them, under a weather that turns every hour and a half or two through every preset but the eerie one; and `freecam`, explore with the camera freed from the start. The mode is the world's, so it travels as a world entry of the address's script, which a follower takes from the host's address; a hike started on a saved choice writes it there. The tier is decided when the game launches and not again: during a hike nothing measures the frames to change it (the governor, `governor.ts`, which lowered Auto's tier live after 30 s under 48 fps, is switched off by `GOVERNOR_ENABLED`, since a change in the middle of a hike covers play and rebuilds the renderer), and only the player changes it. A tier chosen mid-hike is applied at once: the renderer is disposed and rebuilt on a fresh canvas behind the pause screen (`rendererSwap.ts`), while the session and its connections carry on. Every tier draws with Babylon Lite on WebGPU, in any browser whose adapter has what the scene needs: a browser that offers no WebGPU, no adapter, a software adapter or one short of a requirement is told so on a page of its own before anything of a hike starts (`loadCheck.ts`, `turnedAway.ts`). A renderer makes its own engine from the canvas the page hands it, at the start, for each step of the start's measurement and for every live rebuild, and lets it go with itself. A GPU error or a lost device never reloads the page, which would end a party: the hike rebuilds its renderer on a fresh canvas behind a cover (`engineFailure.ts`), tells the telemetry what the device said (`gpuFaults.ts`), and ends with its line at the third lost device inside ten minutes (`lossBound.ts`).

The steep faces and the ground painted rock carry patches over the height field (`engine/src/game/facePatches.ts`, `lite/terrainMeshes.ts`, `lite/patchPool.ts`): a finer grid of the same ground over each 16 m cell with steep or rock-painted ground in reach, each vertex pushed out along the ground's normal by a rock relief with exact derivatives (`engine/src/sim/faceRelief.ts`, pure, no stage of the height chain), and drawn with the terrain's own material, so the paint, the wet layer, the rain, the reflections and the shadows are the ground's. A face, from a gradient of 1.0 to 1.4, takes columns or jointed blocks on tilted slabs, up to 4 m out, and the clipmap leaves its own triangles out under fully steep squares; the walkable rock, wherever the surface paints rock and the face's mask leaves off, takes plates (tilted planes stepping to each other at sharp joints) with shards standing out of them over bedding, gathered into patches by a slow noise, up to a metre out, louder the wider the rock about the cell; the player stands on the rock's walking twin, the same plates with wide joints and no shards, which the simulation's ground field adds over the height field where the surface paints rock (`engine/src/sim/rockSurface.ts`), smooth to walk and one surface with the drawn rock on a plate's top, the rock's edge a ramp by a slope smoothed over three metres, and a scramble: where the layer stands the player may stand on ground up to about 63 degrees (`ROCK_STAND_NORMAL_Y`), the plates' ramps (`docs/rendering/2026-10-12-steep-face-patches-design.md`, `docs/rendering/2026-10-13-walkable-rock-relief-design.md`). The patches build over frames as a patient job, the near band at once, the rest a cell at a time, each cell's sampling cut into slices by the scheduler's clock.

One mute button stands on every page, the title screen, the films and the hike alike (`engine/src/game/muteButton.ts`, on the body, kept in localStorage): pressed, or its key `M` pressed anywhere but in a form field, the hike's ambient master is held at silence (`ambientAudio.ts`) and the intro film's track with it. It sits at the top left corner on every page, the Pause button under it over a hike, where it is as faint as the touch controls are; for a keyboard it carries its key beside the speaker, as the Pause button and the lamp's hint carry theirs. The HUD shows a keyboard player the headlamp's hint, the torch and its key, lit with the lamp (`hud.ts`); a touch screen has its own lamp button instead. Play starts the intro film in its own click, so it plays with sound, and the hike loads and builds behind it (`client/src/game/introOverlay.ts`, over two pure models: `introPlayback.ts`, what a hold, the film's end and the gestures mean, and `loadProgress.ts`, what the loading bar and its line say). Three stages report to the bar (`loadReport.ts`): the models' counts every model a renderer fetches, told by its file as the download starts, as its bytes are in and as the model is made or fails (`lite/forestModels.ts`, `lite/skinnedModels.ts`, `lite/sceneActor.ts`), of a total that is the count of the models asked for so far, whatever the tier, the world and the hikers make that; the ground's and sound's counts the ground's eighteen images (`lite/groundTextures.ts`) and the wildlife's six calls (`wildlifeAudio.ts`); a load that outlives its hike tells the next hike's bar nothing; the world's counts the clipmap's rings, the forest's bakes and the first builds (`engine/src/app.ts`); the bar is weighed by bytes where they are known and by count where they are not, never moves backward, and reads full once the world is ready. The start (`frameProbe.ts`, `startHike`) waits for the film to have six seconds in hand, or three to pass, before the download begins, and lets the page paint before the engine is made and before the build; under the film the clipmap's first build is deferred and run a slice at a time with a paint between (`Renderer.buildFirstClipmap`), and the render loop starts once the rings stand. The world is ready when the renderer's world is in (`Renderer.whenReady`) and the first builds stepped under the film are done (`startReady.ts`, a minute at most); then `hold to skip` shows, and the cut, on a full hold or the step-out click after the title card, lifts the hike's cover and takes the pointer on that gesture. A film that fails, or a join that fails, starts the hike as one with no film does. The title page builds nothing: no renderer, no model, no map is fetched before Play. Behind its title and buttons it shows a still, the first frame of a 30 s silent loop of the world recorded from the scene route's title scene (`scene/title.ts`), and the loop itself once it can play through: it is fetched only after the page has loaded, kept back for a visitor who saves data, is on a slow connection or prefers reduced motion, and stopped, with its download, at Play (`titleLoop.ts`, over the pure `titleLoopModel.ts`).

The sky is one model of the air's light (`engine/src/game/skyModel.ts`): Rayleigh, aerosol and ozone scattering over a spherical planet, from which a module worker builds, at load, a transmittance table, a multiple-scattering table and, for 93 sun altitudes from 18° below the horizon to the noon sun's height, a 64 × 32 slice of the clear sky's radiance (`sky.worker.ts`, `skyWorker.ts`); the app keeps the table for its life, across tier swaps, and nothing is revealed before the slices the noon and the current hour need are in (`lite/worldLight.ts`, `lite/renderer.ts`, `engine/src/app.ts`). A pure state (`skyState.ts`) blends the two slices either side of the sun and turns them, with the weather, into every value the scene reads from the sky: one scale fixed so the clear noon zenith keeps the luminance of the dome it replaced, an adaptation factor standing in for the eye as the light falls through twilight, a night factor where the moonlight takes over, a CIE overcast deck that cloud cover mixes in and whose brightness follows the clear light, the sun's colour and strength from the air's transmittance, the fill, and the horizon's colours away from and toward the sun. The dome (`lite/skyDome.ts`) draws that composition from the blended slice sent as a half-float texture, the dome is drawn into a cube of the scene's own for every PBR material's image-based light, in linear light and at one level, again for each new sky state (`lite/skyCapture.ts`), and the fog colour, the clear colour and the air's gradient and glow take the same horizon colours, so the haze meets the dome at the horizon.

The look is an identity layer over PBR: a material plugin every lit material wears (`engine/src/game/lite/atmospherePlugin.ts`) lays height fog, a distance gradient, sun inscatter and, since 2026-10-15, an aerial perspective by channel: the air's transmittance raised to a power a channel, so the blue scatters in over the first kilometre and the red is left to the horizon; and, since 2026-10-16, a ground haze in every weather, a thin layer at the walker's level that halves every six metres of height, so the far ground sits in a faint, warm veil and the crowns in less of it (`atmosphereParams.ts`). On the high and medium tiers the chain starts with the far softening (`lite/postWgsl.ts`, run by `lite/postChain.ts`): past 80 m of camera depth the frame is blurred by a radius rising to 5 texels at 450 m, each read kept only where it is not much nearer than the texel, so the far field's cards and rims melt into a mass and the near grass stays sharp against it; its depth is the scene's own, resolved by a pass of four reads a pixel into one half float, so nothing is drawn twice. At night the same pass marches the ground cloud once a pixel, along each pixel's ray to that depth, where every material's fragment stage marched it before (2026-10-18, [the frame-rate pass](docs/rendering/2026-10-18-frame-rate-pass-design.md)). The temporal anti-aliasing follows (2026-10-28): the scene is drawn each frame with the projection moved by a fraction of a pixel along an eight-step Halton walk, and the frame is blended into the history of the frames before it, at a tenth while the camera moves and at three hundredths while it is still (so the jitter's swing on a trunk thinner than a pixel does not come through), found where each pixel's point was last frame by that same depth and the last frame's camera, and held to the frame's own 3 by 3 neighbourhood so what moves of itself does not trail, then sharpened on its way down the chain; it settles the leaf cards' alpha-tested edges fluttering against the sky (and settled the shadow map's texel pops under the explore day's fast sun while the sun had a shadow), which neither the multisampling nor FXAA could ([temporal anti-aliasing](docs/rendering/2026-10-28-temporal-anti-aliasing.md)). The paint follows (a generalized Kuwahara at the tier's stroke): at half the frame's pixels on each axis, brought back to the full frame edge by edge by a joint bilateral upsample (the compose, which is the grade's first statement) and mixed over the scene by its strength. On the high tier only, the chain adds the halation's three quarter-size passes, the extract and the two blurs, each blur taking its step at quarter resolution so the glow comes out round rather than stretched along one axis; the grade follows on both tiers (AgX tone map, a night white point, a night Purkinje shift, the split-tone grade, vignette, halation, and last the dither that keeps the 8-bit frame from banding), and medium goes from the paint to the grade with no halation. On the tiers that paint, the frame is drawn at 87 % and then 75 % of the window's pixels on each axis while the frame rate stays low, and back at full once it has room (`renderScale.ts`). Every value is a uniform computed by the pure `*Params.ts` modules from the weather, the hour and `/unsettle`; the low tier has no passes and carries the same intent in its materials, the colour curves, the vignette and the dither at the air's plugin's last point (`materialGrade.ts`, `lite/displayCurve.ts`); the haunt's shades and the Hollow are drawn into a silhouette mask on every tier (`lite/shadeMask.ts`), which the grade pass reads back as a blurred darkness where there is a chain and which the shade veil draws where there is none (`lite/shadeVeil.ts`): one full-screen triangle multiplied into the frame after the scene, carrying the grade's shade and stare blocks, hidden while nothing is in the mask and the stare is at rest ([the phone's measurement](docs/rendering/2026-11-05-low-tier-material-path.md)). Airborne motes (`moteField.ts`, `lite/motes.ts`) follow the sun's altitude and the wind. One wind field (`engine/src/game/windParams.ts`), on the sim's tick so every player's gusts agree, moves the ground cover, the crowns, the motes, the mist and the rain, and swells the ambient wind bed; the foliage's plugin (`foliageUniforms.ts`, `lite/forestPlugins.ts`) also tints every card to the ground it stands on from a per-instance attribute the clutter rebuild writes. The grass floor is hex-tiled from the 2 m grass texture (`groundHexParams.ts`, `lite/terrainPlugins.ts`), gains a finer normal and a between-blades occlusion near the eye, carries a lush-to-dry macro tint the tufts share, and inside the blade field's reach is pulled toward a shaded thatch by the ground cover each terrain vertex carries. The trail is a footpath sunk into the sim's ground (`sim/trail.ts`), painted per fragment from a two-row segment table as four bands that wander with the lattice noise, wear along their length and go wet with the weather (`trailPaint.ts`, `trailBenchParams.ts`), reading as neglected: leaf and needle drifts painted where the ground cover's own duff lies thick, gravel washed out to bare dirt in patches of the bed's own noise, litter along its margin, and the grass beside it only lightly trampled at rebuild. Inside 18 m on the high and medium tiers the near field is opaque blade clumps built in code, placed independently of the cards: `bladeField.ts` walks a half-metre lattice and asks the sim's own grass gate how much grass belongs at each cell, so blades stand wherever the floor is grass rather than only where a meadow card happens to sit, and a cell's strength thins the clump continuously instead of flipping it on or off. The meadow's near cards stand under them as the cover, with the blades as the detail on top, dithering in from a metre out so none stands as a flat plane at the feet. Four characters (fine grass, tussock, broad-leaf weed and a flower-bearing one) cross three distance tiers and three clump sizes into the thirty-six buckets of `lite/bladeMeshes.ts`, one thin-instance draw each on three tier materials, with the coarsest tier reaching the cards' own 18 m seam. A thin-instanced mesh is drawn whole or not at all, and these buckets surround the eye, so the blade buckets and the grass class's card buckets keep their full instance lists and draw only the prefix inside a frustum widened by 6° and pushed back a metre, turned with the camera's roll, cut again after a rebuild or once the camera has turned 3.5°, rolled a degree or moved half a metre (`grassCull.ts`), with only that prefix sent; the low tier draws its grass cards whole. `bladeClump.ts` builds each character as strips of blades; a four-edge collapse in the vertex shader grows a blade in from its tier's inner edge and shrinks it back to its root at the outer one, so neighbouring tiers hand over without a visible seam. The first pass of the post chain is multisampled (`MSAA_SAMPLES`) on those tiers; the low tier draws no blades. The near field reads one ground-cover field in the sim (`groundCover`, `sim/clutter.ts`) that thins grass toward every non-grass neighbour and thickens it inside, and where it thins a second lattice (`duffField.ts`, `duffClump.ts`, `lite/duffMeshes.ts`) lays code-built dead leaves, twigs and branches on the floor, whose paint follows the same field; a cell buys the clump size its cover earns, dithered so no contour forms. The low tier gets the paint only. The understory is two more of the sim's clutter classes, placed by habitat (`sim/clutter.ts`): sword fern, the forest's own fern model in clumps a metre or two across, in patches under the canopy on low ground, a trace in the open and thinning uphill; and an evergreen shrub layer in thickets, as salal behind the beach and on drained slopes under the lowland canopy and as huckleberry and heath from the high forest to the treeline. The shrub has no model: its mound of oval leaves on arching stems is built in code (`shrubClump.ts`), two characters at two levels of detail, with its reflection all but taken away, since a mound of glossy facets mirrors the sky and reads grey. A fallen log is seated by its trunk and not its bounding box (`logSeat.ts`): a line fitted through the model's underside, robust to the root flare under it, is laid on a line fitted through the ground at five places along the log, and the log is sunk a fifth of its diameter into the floor. A log is 9 to 17 m long and over a metre through, and the sim lets none lie across a trail or the road: a tree that would fall across either stands dead instead (`logCrossesPath`, `sim/vegetation.ts`). A log is solid: the trees pass (`sim/passes/trees.ts`) lays a chain of square boxes along its trunk, each from the ground under its station to most of the trunk's height, so a hiker climbs over a log or goes round it and never through it; a log that crosses a chunk border is laid from both chunks, each keeping the boxes that lie mostly inside it. A dead tree, log or snag, wears a trunk's bark set and the trunks' moss (`engine/src/game/deadwoodParams.ts`, `lite/forestPlugins.ts`): the set's coordinates are laid along the trunk and round it in the vertex stage at the instance's scale, a whole number of tiles round, so the plates stay life-size on a trunk drawn three times its model; the moss lies on a log's upper side by the world normal, mossier than any standing trunk, and down a snag's wet side low on the trunk as on a living one; a log's bark is darkened toward its underside where it meets the floor, and the whole is toned down to weathered wood. One log in two is drawn mirrored, which turns its winding, so the material draws both faces lit one-sided, and on the high tier the near bucket draws the file's full mesh. Seven logs in ten are nurse logs, each with a few ferns and conifer seedlings along its top, placed from the log's own hash on the log's own matrix (`nurseLog.ts`); the seedling is built in code. The forest plants nothing of its own at a tree's foot: the fern and the shrub it once stood there, one a tree and scaled with it, are retired for the two clutter classes. Wet ground has its own class: skunk cabbage and devil's club, both built in code (`wetPlantClump.ts`), along seeps (winding ribbons, the middle band of a low-frequency noise, under the lowland canopy on ground no steeper than a bench) and in a ring round a lake outside its bare shore. The high ground has its own too: beargrass (most of it tussocks, some under a tall flowering stalk), lupine and mountain heather, built in code (`highPlantClump.ts`), in drifts from the high forest up to the snow and mostly in the open. The one broadleaf tree is an alder, a third species of the regeneration cohort with its own model: it stands where `alderHabitat` (`sim/vegetation.ts`) says the ground is its own, along the road's verge, round a lake outside its tree margin and along the seeps (`sim/seep.ts`, the field the wet-ground plants read too), on lowland ground; there most cells hold a tree whatever the forest's own density is, and most of those trees are alders. Along the top of the beach lies a drift line, a clutter class of the same trunk bleached and laid along the shore, seated the same way. Rocks and boulders are cut into fractured stone at load (`rockRelief.ts`) — seeded planes, flat facets, a per-facet tone — four ways per model, with instances spread across the cuts by hash. The cut only ever removes material, so it stays inside the collision boxes the simulation declares and changes nothing in `sim/`; where a plane cleaves a boulder's top the box, sized from the uncut height, is left standing up to 0.30 m above the visible stone (`docs/rendering/2026-09-23-rock-relief-design.md`, §3). Rock ground too steep to stand on is dressed with two instanced rock-wall models. Where they stand is decided in the simulation (`sim/cliffField.ts`), because a wall is part of the world every peer must agree on, so its constants are in the level id: on a 12 m lattice, one cell in ten whose jittered point is steep rock lays a run of modules along the contour, each module wherever its centre and the ground under its whole above-ground body — the corners of that box and the edge midpoints of its longer axes, the top edge among them — are all rock and below the simulation's stand limit by a margin, so a module never stands where a player can, facing downslope, leaning toward the ground normal but by no more than 20°, so it stands against the hillside instead of lying back along it, and sunk a third of its height. The renderer (`cliffField.ts`, `lite/cliffMeshes.ts`) draws them in three LOD rings out to 400 m on the high tier, each tinted halfway toward the ground under it (`cliffInstances.ts`). Each module is solid: a chunk pass (`sim/passes/cliffs.ts`) lays a row of axis-aligned boxes along it, one for each piece of at most 4 m of its length, that together contain the whole drawn module, the part the sink buries included, each with its uphill face moved back into the hill until the hillside reaches its top (so a hiker sliding down behind a wall lands on it rather than being caught against it), so a wall stops a hiker and blocks sight the way a boulder does.

The water is one blended surface a body, the sea's lattice (`engine/src/game/lite/seaMeshes.ts`, `lite/seaPlugin.ts`) and a lake's grid on the row its murk gives (`lite/lakeMeshes.ts`, `lite/lakePlugin.ts`, `lakeWaterRow`), a PBR material with a plugin whose shading is one text for both (`lite/waterWgsl.ts`): it reads the bed's depth per pixel from a camera-local height texture baked from the sim's `elevationAt` a row a frame into a spare grid and swapped in whole (`bedHeight.ts`, `lite/waterBed.ts`), discards on land so the mesh's vertices on land draw no pixel, and transmits the bed by the attenuation measured for each body (`waterShading.ts`: Kd per channel, the deep-water colour, and the shelter that scales Cox and Munk's wind roughness). It holds the reflected ray above the horizon, and on the high tier it is drawn opaque over the frame the opaque draws left: the scene's pass is broken before it on a frame that shows it, and it reads that frame's colour and depth (`lite/waterBehind.ts`), so what stands in the water is attenuated by its own depth. A lake is glass or ripples, not a blur between: the share of its surface that is glass is read from a table of the hour and the weather's preset (`lakeCalm.ts`), all of it from 18:00 to 08:00 under a clear, bright or eerie sky, ramping to none by 09:00 and back from 17:00; under mist all of it by night and 0.3 by day; under overcast 0.3 at the day's ends and none by day; none in rain. The rest carries cat's-paws that cross it with the wind's gusts, or, in rain or a hard wind on an exposed lake, all of it is rough with the wind's roughness. The ripples have two states (`lakeRipples.ts`): the paws, patches of the gust-raised field that cover the share the calm leaves, as far as the lake's exposure lets them, and drift downwind on the shared clock, and the rain's rings, drawn analytically within 8 m of the eye. Where the glass lies the lake reflects its shore in place of the sky probe, through PBR's own Fresnel: on the high tier a camera mirrored in the water's plane, its near plane made the water's by an oblique projection rather than a clip plane (`mirrorView.ts`), draws a short list of cheap draws into a target of half the frame's size, 960 × 540 at most, every third frame while the lake is in view and the glass shows, its field a fifth wider than the player's and its read smeared on every frame by the eye's travel over the two frames between draws (`mirrorPass.ts`, `lite/lakeMirror.ts`); on the medium tier a panorama of the shore is captured from the lake's centre in sixteen sectors, a frame each, whenever the sky's probe is re-armed (`panoramaTurn.ts`, `lite/lakePanorama.ts`), and the treeline's elevation by azimuth (`lakeSkyline.ts`), read from the lake's rim in each direction (the farthest crossing of its outline), masks the panorama's sky there and, on the low tier, stands in for the shore alone; the water reads both on one cylinder about the centre, of the lake's mean radius. One mirror and one panorama serve the lake nearest the camera, moving to another once it is 20 m nearer (`lakeChoice.ts`); every lake has its own skyline. The sea has none of this. A lake's surface is a 2 m grid over its box, kept within 3 m of its shore. Its shore distance on the terrain is baked at load into a shore atlas, 128 × 128 texels a lake over its bound's square, held as rows of the feature table's float texture (`shoreAtlas.ts`, `featureTable.ts`, `terrainTables.ts`); the ground paint's shore tint and the wet line read it. A second plugin (`wetSurface.ts`, `lite/wetWgsl.ts`) darkens and glosses the ground, the props and the characters below a body's wet line, inside that body's footprint: on the terrain a lake's shore by the atlas, elsewhere a disc to its farthest shore point. The band the sea's swash wets on the cove is laid from that body's level too, and the cove is the sea's: under a lake's wet line the look is given no cove (`wetUniforms`), so no lake's bank carries the band. A murky lake carries a skin of duckweed and algae in the same plugin, laid from its depth, its murk and a seeded noise, and its reeds, cattails and pond-lilies are built in code (`waterPlantShapes.ts`) and drawn by `lite/waterPlantMeshes.ts` from the sim's clutter classes. The sea moves (`client/src/game/ocean*.ts`, `lite/seaWgsl.ts`). A swell seeded per world, inside the real coast's range of height, period and direction, is a sum of twelve components (eight on the low tier) in pairs close in frequency, so it arrives in sets; each component's phase is integrated along the coast from tables built at load from the bed's own profiles (`oceanTables.ts`), blending from the bay's to the cove's over 250 m past the cove's ends, so a wave slows, steepens and turns toward the beach as the bed rises, and a rule by the depth under it (Weggel's breaker index) caps it and spills it into a bore and its white water across the cove's bed (`oceanWaves.ts`), sheltered behind the headlands. The tables, the components and the coastline reach the GPU as one float texture, which the sea's plugin reads in the vertex stage, displacing seven stitched rings from 1 m and handing the swell's normal, height and envelope on, and in the fragment stage, for the break and the foam from that envelope. A wind sea from the weather and the hour, following the wind a minute behind and, under an offshore wind, held near the shore to the Coastal Engineering Manual's fetch law, rides on the swell, with whitecaps by Callaghan's coverage: a GPU FFT of three cascades in compute on the high tier (`oceanFft.ts`, `lite/seaFft.ts`), a 20 s loop baked in a worker at load on the medium tier (`oceanLoopBake.ts`), the bump's normals on the low tier. The sea runs on the sim's tick (`sharedSeconds`, `oceanWindSea.ts`), so every player sees the same crest at the same moment, and `crestAt` and `boreArrivals` give the shore's later work each wave's height, period and phase.

The sea's edge is on the cove's pebble face, and it is render-side throughout: nothing of it is in the simulation or crosses the network, and everything of it moves on the sea's shared seconds and the swell the page draws, so players on the same tier see the same wave plunge and hear the same thud (the low tier's swell has eight components, the others' twelve). A crest that reaches the face's toe unbroken plunges there by the Iribarren number of the 1:12 face, less as the wind turns onshore, and its progress is the break rule's own ratio of height to depth (`oceanBreaker.ts`): on the high tier, while the FFT draws the wind sea, a strip of geometry swept along the toe and drawn with the sea's material carries the curl, the lip thrown over a tube from a baked cross-section (`lipStrip.ts`, `lite/lipMeshes.ts`), and everywhere else (the medium and low tiers, and the high tier without the FFT) the sea's fragment stage steepens the face, lights the crest line and bursts the foam (`lite/seaWgsl.ts`); the two never draw together. Each bore at the toe sends a sheet up the face by Hunt's rule, held to a cap that lobes along the shore (`swashRunUp.ts`), that thins as it sinks into the pebbles; a table of 512 columns, one a metre of shore, holds each column's front, thickness, reach and the time since it was last wet, refilled each frame (`swashTable.ts`), from which the sea lifts its surface into the sheet and the wet ground (`wetSurface.ts`) draws its line, reading the table as a uniform array, soaked, then damp, then dry over a minute, a faint speckle of foam riding the band after each retreat; the bays keep the still line. The same table and the breaker's plunges fill one record a frame for the surf's sound (`surfSound.ts`), voiced beside the lake's life (`surfAudio.ts`): a recorded bed that swells with the sets and the swell's height, a thud for each plunge and a rattle for each backwash, low-passed inland and under the canopy and joined to the world bus, so the stare's muffle and the reveal's hush act on it. On the high tier each plunge also throws a short burst of spray, thin-instanced sprites placed in the vertex stage by a closed-form throw under gravity, drag and the wind (`sprayField.ts`, `lite/surfSpray.ts`), six bursts at most, drawn among the see-through effects. All of it is made with the renderer's sea (`lite/renderer.ts`, `lite/seaMeshes.ts`) and goes with it on a change of tier.

The lake has its own life (`engine/src/game/waterLifeScene.ts`, drawn by `lite/waterLifeMeshes.ts`), render-side like the animals: where it lives is placed once from the world's seed and its lake (`waterLifeField.ts`), the midges' swarm markers over the shore's shrubs, reeds, driftlogs and snags and over open water, the darners' beats along the shore, each laid by its length along the lake's outline, the skimmers' perches, the damselflies' stems in the beds of reeds and wet plants and the chorus frogs' voices at the water's edge; a perch on land stands on its own ground, and only a reed's perch or a stem over water stands on the water. What is there follows the hour, the weather, the wind and the dread (`waterLifeParams.ts`), each share eased over 3 s: the midges from 05:15 to 07:00 and across dusk, up from 17:45, full from 18:15 to 19:15 and gone by a quarter to eight, out of a strong wind and the rain; the dragonflies by day in sun, the darners flying on into dusk at a third of their share until 19:00; in a strong wind the darners take cover while the skimmers and the damselflies sit perched; under a cloud only the skimmers, on their perches; the frogs from dusk until before dawn; and none of it by the middle of the dread. The midges are one thin-instanced draw of camera-facing specks (`midgeLook.ts`, `lite/midgeMeshes.ts`) with no texture read: each midge's place about its swarm's centre is three sinusoids an axis from its own seed and the shared seconds (`midgeMotion.ts`), so the swarms move alike on every player's screen and no per-midge data is sent a frame, only one buffer of a few values and a table of 32 rows, each row a swarm's centre after the wind's shift and surge, its size, its count and its presence. The rows are the markers and one over each player who has stood still in the shore band for 10 s, and the tier's midges in view (800, 2,000 and 4,000) are shared among them by size and distance, a far swarm thinning to a tenth at 60 m and gone beyond 80 m; close to the eye a midge's coverage never falls below a floor, which fades out from 6 to 15 m, so a far swarm is faint specks. On the low tier, with no post chain, the midges' stage tones their light with the frame's exposure, tone map, encode and contrast, as the sky dome's does. The motes keep pollen and frost only. The dragonflies are a few dozen small state machines on the CPU (`dragonflyBehaviour.ts`), the darners beating along the shore, the skimmers sallying from their perches and back and the damselflies hopping from stem to stem in their bed, drawn as thin-instanced cards, one draw a kind, whose wings shimmer through the wing plugin (`lite/dragonflyMeshes.ts`, `lite/wingPlugin.ts`). Midges and dragonflies draw among the rain, the mist and the motes, in the water's group on the high tier. The sound goes through looping positional sources in the ambient graph (`ambientAudio.ts`): the nearest swarms' hum, synthesized at a pitch that follows the summer's temperature, the dragonflies' wings, and the frogs' calls, which fall silent around each player and near the Hollow and, once players have left a voice, are heard from it again only after its own 20 to 40 s (`frogChorus.ts`, `waterLifeAudio.ts`, `insectVoices.ts`). The calls are recordings, each voice its own single call at its own rate (the synthesized call while its clip is not in, or if it fails to load), over a far chorus of two loops across the water that thins while a player stands among the near frogs, falls quiet around a player as the voices do, and goes silent with the dread and the Hollow. All of it is mixed into the ambient graph's world bus, which reaches the master through the stare's low-pass and then a brick-wall limiter, so the frogs, a hum over the head and a rustle together cannot clip. Every lake has its own life, placed at load and stepped only while the camera stands within 250 m past its bound (`lakeChoice.ts`), and the life heard is the lake the reflections serve; with the camera out of reach of all of it, nothing is stepped.

The people are models from one character pool (`engine/src/game/lite/skinnedModels.ts`, drawn by `lite/entityMeshes.ts`): every other player is one of five rangers, chosen by player id so every peer sees the same one, and the Hollow is `hollow.antlered`, drawn at twice a player's height over an unchanged hull, its clip following its measured speed and its eyes lit a saturated emissive red, the tell that reads from far off (`hollowLook.ts`). The things placed once and seen up close — the car and the board at the trailhead (`sceneryParts.ts`, `lite/sceneryMeshes.ts`; the board's face is a plane painted when the match starts, `boardDraw.ts`: the trail's name routed into the wood, and a map of the world's own trails, the missing hiker's poster and a sheet of rules on paper), the body at the crest (`sceneryParts.ts`) and the fingerposts, one plank per place name painted on thin planes, each plank at a height of its own on a post stretched to hold them (`signParts.ts`) — are static models loaded then placed with only their `LOD0` drawn (`lite/sceneryModels.ts`). Each stands on the box the simulation collides with, and a model that has not loaded, or never will, leaves a stand-in drawn in its place: a capsule for a hiker or the Hollow, the sim's own boxes for the car, the board and a sign post, a timber cross for the body. No collider is ever invisible.

Rain is a camera-locked wrapped volume, not a particle emitter (`engine/src/game/rainField.ts`, `rainParams.ts`, drawn by `lite/rain.ts`): one thin-instanced quad mesh holds a fixed population of streaks (6,000, 20,000 and 48,000 by tier), each a seed in the unit cube that the vertex stage places by a fold of its seed inside a 24 by 20 by 24 m box biased 6 m along the view, so the box is full at every height on every frame, the field of drops stands still in the world while the box slides over it, and nothing is uploaded per frame; four fall-speed classes slant by the wind, the streak's length is the motion blur of a smoothed frame time floored at a fortieth of a second (the eye's persistence, so a fast machine draws no shorter streak), its alpha fades toward the sky and brightens in the headlamp's cone, and the fog's density and greyness follow the rain (`weather.ts`). On the medium and high tiers a top-down height map (`rainCover.ts`, `lite/rainMap.ts`: a 512-texel target over 96 m around the player, drawn again once each time the player has moved 8 m, of the two inner terrain rings, the props, the cliffs and the water through three materials of the map's own) stores each texel's top height, its transmission (open ground 1, canopy by the terrain's own density, props and cliffs 0) and a ceiling lift; the streaks read it and stop under hard cover but fall whole under the canopy, splash rings (`splashField.ts`, `lite/rainSplash.ts`) land on its height around the player, and a sparse vertical drip volume falls only under canopy, driven by a canopy-water value that fills a minute into the rain and drains over ten (`canopyWaterStep` in `weather.ts`), which the ambient audio's drip layer hears as well (`ambientAudio.ts`). Every PBR material given a porosity cap (the trees, the understory, the clutter, the cliffs, the props; the terrain keeps its own trail-paint wetness) darkens and glosses with the weather's wetness by a porosity rule (`wetSurface.ts`), the trail's puddles carry four layers of rings hashed in the fragment behind a branch on the rain (`trailPaint.ts`), and on the tiers with the post chain a `lens` pass after the grade refracts the frame through a code-generated droplet field and a few sliding drops, frosting toward a four-tap blur of the scene, strongest looking up into the rain and never under 60 percent of it, and skipped while idle (`lensParams.ts`, `lite/postWgsl.ts`). The measured costs are in [the rain system's verification](docs/rendering/2026-10-01-rain-system-verification.md).

## Assets

- `client/assets/models/*.glb`, `client/assets/textures/*.webp`, `client/assets/textures/*.ktx2` and `client/assets/audio/*.mp3` are committed through Git LFS.
- `client/assets/catalog.json` has three lists: `assets` (models — `id`, `kind` of `character`, `creature`, `environment` or `prop`, `output`, and for animated models an `animations` map from the role the game asks for (`idle`, `walk`, `attack`, `death`, `graze`, `run`, `alert`) to the clip name in the file), `textures` (the ground layers, and the trees' bark sets as KTX2 — `id`, `output`, `bytes`), and `audio` (wildlife calls — `id`, `output`).
- The trees' bark is not their files': each tree's bark material is dressed as its model lands in one of four scanned bark sets (`engine/src/game/barkParams.ts`, `docs/rendering/2026-10-14-tree-bark-textures.md`), tiled in metres, the giant pine in two mixed tree by tree.
- Assets are imported with Vite `?url` globs (`engine/src/game/assetUrls.ts`), so production URLs are content-hashed and cached as immutable. A catalog `output` with no file behind it throws at startup rather than 404ing into a missing mesh.
- `CREDITS.md` credits third-party work; `client/src/game/credits.ts` parses it for the in-game Credits screen.

### Model conventions

What the game assumes of every model file:

- Units are metres; the origin is at the model's feet (or base), and forward is +Z.
- Models streamed by distance have `LOD0`, `LOD1` and `LOD2` root nodes, each holding that level's meshes; the game finds them by those names.
- Clip names are the ones the catalog's `animations` map gives.
- A part the game repaints is found by its material: the Hollow's eyes are its one material with a non-zero emissive factor. The trailhead board is placed by its numbers instead: the board's face 2 m by 1 m toward +Z, its centre 1.37 m above the board's foot and 0.159 m in front of its centre plane, which is where the game stands its own painted plane.
- Saturated red belongs to the Hollow's eyes alone (`HOLLOW_EYE_COLOR`, `engine/src/game/hollowLook.ts`), so its tell never camouflages against the world. It is a convention for every model and material, not a check: no test enforces it.

## Hosting

The site is on Firebase Hosting at `games.csarko.sh/dayhike`; the signaling server is a Cloud Run service whose rooms live in Firestore, shared by its instances, so it scales from zero to a few and back (`docs/signaling/`). The telemetry service is the same image in its other role on a second Cloud Run service that scales from zero, writing rows to a Cloud Logging bucket of their own with Log Analytics on. Terraform in `_infra/` owns all of it, plus DNS. `tools/deploy/` builds and ships them and verifies production independently.

## Testing

Vitest runs three roots — `client`, `server`, `tools` — all headless. What draws is tested with no device: the modules that compute with no engine run under Node, the WGSL is read as text, and the hike's renderer is held as it stands before its engine does; `client/test/game/catalogModels.test.ts` reads every catalog model with the game's own reader and checks its nodes, clips and vertices.
