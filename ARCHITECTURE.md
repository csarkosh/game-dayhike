# Architecture

## Layers

`client/src` has three layers with one direction of dependency, `game/ → net/ → sim/`:

- **`sim/`** — the deterministic simulation: movement, collision, interact, AI, and the seeded world (terrain, forest, clutter, trails). Pure TypeScript with no Babylon, no DOM and no wall-clock reads. ESLint forbids `sim/` importing Babylon, `net/` or `game/`, and `client/test/architecture.test.ts` names any file that does.
- **`net/`** — the versioned binary protocol, the WebRTC transport, and the host and client sessions. The host is authoritative; clients predict their own input and reconcile against host snapshots.
- **`game/`** — Babylon.js: the scene, meshes, materials and shaders, camera, HUD, audio and input sampling. It reads `sim/` state and never writes it except through input commands. Ambient wildlife placement lives here rather than in `sim/`: it is cosmetic, so peers need not agree on it byte-for-byte.

`server/` is the signaling server (Node, `ws`): it introduces peers in a room and steps out. `desktop/` is an Electron launcher that loads the hosted site.

## Determinism

The simulation runs at a fixed 60 Hz from a seeded RNG. The world is derived from the level id and seed alone, so every peer builds the same world locally and nothing about it crosses the wire. A test serializes two runs of the world from identical inputs and asserts they match; client prediction depends on it. The trail bench's sink and width and the litter class are part of the level id through their tunables, so a client on an older build cannot join a newer host.

The match's two acts follow the same split. The one missing hiker the poster names, the place at the crest where the body lies (`client/src/sim/search.ts`), the car at the pad, the trailhead board at the trail's entrance, turned to face where a player arrives, with the poster on its face (`client/src/sim/trailhead.ts`), where a player arrives and the way they face — in front of the car, toward the trail's entrance (`client/src/sim/spawn.ts`) — the sign posts at the trail's forks, one plank per place, each place once on a post on the arm with the shortest trail to it and the Summit on top, (`client/src/sim/signs.ts`), with every pond and meadow named from a seeded stream of its own so naming moves no other draw (`client/src/sim/placeNames.ts`), and the wall at the road's edge (`client/src/sim/containment.ts`) are all derived from the seed and the trail graph on every peer. The rules over them are host state (`client/src/sim/summit.ts`): the phase — a climb until the first living player comes within 12 m of the body, a chase from that tick on — whether each player stands on the road corridor, which is safe ground a Hollow never enters, and the match's outcome, which settles when no living player is still out in the woods. The phase, each player's safe bit and the outcome ride every snapshot (protocol 5), and the wall clamps inside the movement step so a client predicts it exactly. That graph is built by `client/src/sim/trailBuild.ts` on the shared routing plumbing in `client/src/sim/trailPlan.ts` — a stem from the pad to the crest with its seeded loops, and below the crest a web of parallel strands cross-linked by rungs (`client/src/sim/trailBraid.ts`) — and carries its junctions and every node's trail distance home as `forks` and `homeDist`.

At the trailhead the forest comes down to the road, and the trail leaves the pad inland. The shore's rules — where trees thin toward the beach, where grass, rocks, fungi and bushes stop, where the ground is painted sand, and the ground the forest's animals keep to — read the ground's height, and at the trailhead they read it through a strip (`client/src/sim/shoreStrip.ts`): a weight that is a function of a place in the road's frame alone, 1 within 30 m of the pad along the road and fading over the next 15 m, from the road's bed inland to 100 m and fading over the next 20 m, and 0 at the road's centreline and seaward of it. `shoreHeight` is the ground's height plus 9 m times the weight, so inside the strip the rules read forest ground; the forest's density is at least the weight itself there, times the road's own gate. The terrain's own heights are not changed: the strip moves what grows and what is painted, never the ground's shape, and it is read by `client/src/sim/vegetation.ts`, `client/src/sim/clutter.ts`, `client/src/game/terrainSurface.ts` and `client/src/game/wildlifeField.ts`. The trail's search keeps off the shore (`closeShore` in `client/src/sim/trailGrid.ts`, called by `client/src/sim/trailBuild.ts`): every cell of its grid whose ground is under 9 m or that lies within 30 m of the road's centreline is closed, by the ground's own height and not the strip's, but for the two rows of cells straight inland of the pad, the doorway. It closes cells and opens none, so a doorway cell too steep for the search stays closed. Round the pad is the trailhead's clearing (`TRAILHEAD_CLEARING` in `client/src/sim/clutter.ts`): within 24 m of the pad's centre no rock, boulder, fungus or bush stands, so where a player arrives, the entrance and the board are in the open. The board is judged by the way a player faces as they arrive (`facingDir` in `client/src/sim/facing.ts`, sine and cosine as polynomials, since `sim/` uses no trigonometry of the platform's). The strip's numbers, the search's and the clearing's are part of the level id through their tunables.

The water's ground is the sim's too. The trail's pond is a lake whose murk follows where it lies on the climb from the pad to the crest, with a shore shelf, a drop to a middle as deep as its water is clear, and on a murky lake a marsh on the shelf at one end, its ground held at the water's level (`client/src/sim/features.ts`). The lake is applied to the composed field only, after the bowl is built, so nothing the build places can move, and a wall at the shelf's edge keeps a wading player's camera above the water (`containment.ts`). The shore in front of the pad is a pebble cove between two headlands, seaward of the road's corridor only (`olympic.ts`). The variant lists its water (`waterBodies`: the sea, then each lake) and the cove's weight (`coveMask`) for the renderer, the clutter, the ground paint and the wading.

The Hollow (`client/src/sim/hollow.ts`) is one `EnemyState` with four `AiState` values — Emerge, Hunt, Stand and Watch, the last being the climb's watcher (`client/src/sim/watcher.ts`), which stands off the trail facing the lead (its first two showings stand on the trail itself, up ahead, so no party misses it) and is spawned when it shows and removed when it hides — so the snapshot's existing enemy channel carries it unchanged, no new wire shape needed. Its rules and the routes it walks (`client/src/sim/trailRoute.ts`) run host-only, inside the same authoritative tick as everything else in `sim/`. The cut (`client/src/sim/cut.ts`) — the hidden guide route drawn from the world RNG on the discovery tick, and the forks cut as the party descends — is the same kind of state: `World.cut`, on `World` rather than `WorldState`, host-only and never on the wire; so is the watcher's record (`World.watcher`: whether it is shown, the rest until it shows again, and its own random stream, so the climb never moves the guide); peers only ever receive more Hollows. Each player's stare at it rides the snapshot as one byte, and kills nobody: the player's own screen and ears take it (`client/src/game/stareLens.ts`, `stareAudio.ts`) as a darkness that closes from the Hollow's side on a heartbeat, a world gone muffled, and whispers. The climb has a voice of its own (`client/src/game/woodsVoice.ts`): a birdsong bed that thins with the party's progress and stops dead while the watcher shows, and the Hollow's call from up the trail at six marks of the climb, nearer each time. Death, on every level, is permanent: there is no respawn.

## Rendering

The world streams around the camera in rings: full geometry near, cheaper levels of detail further out, and a far field beyond. What a step over a cell line changes — the terrain's rings, the ground cover's lists, the forest's bands — is rebuilt as a job run in slices over the frames that follow, at most 4 ms of them a frame, its buffers uploaded together in its last slice and never more than six frames or 100 ms after the crossing, whichever comes first (`client/src/game/syncJobs.ts`); the first build, a teleport and the free camera's boost rebuild at once. A terrain ring keeps each vertex's lifted height and normal, so a step samples only the strip it moves onto, which, with the budget jobs leave, it samples ahead onto the line the camera is heading for (`clipmap.ts`); the ground cover's collectors keep their squares of cells and look up only the strip a step adds, which they too look up ahead, as the forest collects ahead the bands of the next cell it is heading into (`cellWindow.ts`). Quality tiers (`low`, `medium`, `high`, `client/src/game/quality.ts`) set hardware scaling, shadow map sizes and LOD bias; the tier is chosen from the GPU the browser names (`gpuSignals.ts`, `gpuClass.ts`), confirmed by a short measurement of the heaviest standard view where the browser will not name it (`frameProbe.ts`), and can be forced with `?tier=low|medium|high`. The player can set the tier in Settings, on the title screen and the pause screen (`settings.ts`); Auto is the default. The tier is decided when the game launches and not again: during a hike nothing measures the frames to change it (the governor, `governor.ts`, which lowered Auto's tier live after 30 s under 48 fps, is switched off by `GOVERNOR_ENABLED`, since a change in the middle of a hike covers play and rebuilds the renderer), and only the player changes it. A tier chosen mid-hike is applied at once: the renderer is disposed and rebuilt on a fresh canvas behind the pause screen (`rendererSwap.ts`), while the session and its connections carry on. The high tier draws with Babylon's WebGPU engine in Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge on macOS or Windows on a device that is not a phone or a tablet, where the adapter has the limits the scene needs: the one tier and browser where WebGPU was measured to draw faster on macOS, and to reach its full rate sooner on a first visit on Windows, Edge being the same engine as Chrome ([the figures](docs/rendering/2026-09-26-webgpu-high-tier-verification.md#8-the-default-switched-on-where-it-was-measured-faster)). Everything else, the medium and low tiers, the desktop launcher, browsers whose brands name neither Chrome nor Edge (a browser is told apart from Chrome only by the brands it reports), Safari, Firefox, Linux and mobile devices, draws with WebGL2. On Auto a machine reaches High, and so WebGPU, only by its GPU's class or by the start's measurement on WebGL2. The engine can be forced with `?engine=webgl2|webgpu` (`client/src/game/engineChoice.ts` holds the rule and the remembered fallback, `gpuEngine.ts` the engine, loaded only by a dynamic import, so a WebGL2 page fetches none of it). The engine is chosen for each renderer from the tier it is for, after the tier is decided: the probe's steps, the hike's start and every live rebuild. Every way WebGPU fails ends on WebGL2 and none reloads the page, which would end a party: before the game starts, in the same start; after it, by a live rebuild of the renderer on a fresh canvas, remembered for the browser and Babylon versions (a first lost device retries once on a new WebGPU engine; a start that runs out of time, which a busy machine does as well as one that cannot run WebGPU, is remembered only from the second inside a day, and then for a day rather than 30). On WebGPU a render pipeline a draw has not met yet is made asynchronously and the mesh left out of the frame until it lands, rather than every frame waiting behind a synchronous creation (`asyncPipelines.ts`, a run-time patch on the engine's own pipeline cache; the scene's rendering groups are its scope, the impostor bake keeps only a whole render, and the start shows the world once a frame leaves nothing out); `?pipelines=sync` keeps Babylon's own path, `?pipelines=<1–16>` sets how many are made at once ([its design](docs/rendering/2026-09-28-webgpu-async-pipelines-design.md)).

Play starts the intro film in its own click, so it plays with sound, and the hike loads and builds behind it (`client/src/game/introOverlay.ts`, over two pure models: `introPlayback.ts`, what a hold, the film's end and the gestures mean, and `loadProgress.ts`, what the loading bar and its line say). Every model passes through one loader (`modelLoad.ts`, `loadContainer`, each site inside its own `loadUntilAborted`), which reports its start, its bytes as they land and its settle to that model, and the ground maps, the albedos and the wildlife calls, the WGSL map of the page's quality tier and the translators' files, the render pipelines and the clipmap's rings report likewise; the bar is weighed by bytes where they are known and by count where they are not, never moves backward and never shows a total it made up. The start (`frameProbe.ts`, `startHike`) waits for the film to have six seconds in hand, or three to pass, before the download begins, and lets the page paint before the engine is made and before the build; under the film the clipmap's first build is deferred and run a slice at a time with a paint between (`Renderer.buildFirstClipmap`), and the render loop starts once the rings stand. The world is ready when the scene is ready with nothing in flight, the forest's billboards are baked and, on WebGPU, a frame has left no draw out (`startReady.ts`, the tier switch's own test run once at the start, a minute at most); then `hold to skip` shows, and the cut, on a full hold or the step-out click after the title card, lifts the hike's cover and takes the pointer on that gesture. A film that fails, or a join that fails, starts the hike as one with no film does. The title page builds nothing: no renderer, no model, no map is fetched before Play. Behind its title and buttons it shows a still, the first frame of a 30 s silent loop of the world recorded from the scene route's title scene (`scene/title.ts`), and the loop itself once it can play through: it is fetched only after the page has loaded, kept back for a visitor who saves data, is on a slow connection or prefers reduced motion, and stopped, with its download, at Play (`titleLoop.ts`, over the pure `titleLoopModel.ts`).

The sky is one model of the air's light (`client/src/game/skyModel.ts`): Rayleigh, aerosol and ozone scattering over a spherical planet, from which a module worker builds, at load, a transmittance table, a multiple-scattering table and, for 93 sun altitudes from 18° below the horizon to the noon sun's height, a 64 × 32 slice of the clear sky's radiance (`sky.worker.ts`, `skyWorker.ts`); the app keeps the table for its life, across tier swaps, and nothing is revealed before the slices the noon and the current hour need are in (`lighting.ts`, `renderer.ts`, `client/src/app.ts`). A pure state (`skyState.ts`) blends the two slices either side of the sun and turns them, with the weather, into every value the scene reads from the sky: one scale fixed so the clear noon zenith keeps the luminance of the dome it replaced, an adaptation factor standing in for the eye as the light falls through twilight, a night factor where the moonlight takes over, a CIE overcast deck that cloud cover mixes in and whose brightness follows the clear light, the sun's colour and strength from the air's transmittance, the fill, and the horizon's colours away from and toward the sun. The dome (`skyDome.ts`, `shaders/skyDome.*.fx`) draws that composition from the blended slice uploaded as a half-float texture, a reflection probe captures it for every PBR material's image-based light (half float where the engine renders it, and gamma-encoded: the dome writes its linear radiance raised to 1/2.2 and each material raises it back), and the fog colour, the clear colour and the atmosphere plugin's gradient and glow take the same horizon colours, so the haze meets the dome at the horizon.

The look is an identity layer over PBR: a material plugin (`client/src/game/atmosphere.ts`) replaces Babylon's fog with height fog, a distance gradient and sun inscatter. On the high tier only, the chain runs `scene` (a full-resolution pass the halation extract reads from) → halation extract and the two blurs, each blur taking its step at quarter resolution (on high `grade` is built at the halation's ratio, because its ratio sizes the vertical blur's write target) so the glow comes out round rather than stretched along one axis → `grade` (AgX tone map, a night white point, a night Purkinje shift, the split-tone grade, vignette, halation) → chromatic aberration and FXAA passes built directly (there is no `DefaultRenderingPipeline`) → `finish` (peripheral overlap, luminance grain, dither); medium drops the halation chain and starts at `grade`, whose ratio there stays 1.0 since it is the first pass and sizes the scene render itself. Every value is a uniform computed by the pure `*Params.ts` modules from the weather, the hour and `/unsettle`; the low tier has no passes and carries the same intent through Babylon's in-material image processing. Airborne motes (`motes.ts`) follow the sun's altitude and the wind. One wind field (`client/src/game/windParams.ts`), on the sim's tick so every player's gusts agree, moves the ground cover, the crowns, the motes, the mist and the rain, and swells the ambient wind bed; the foliage plugin (`foliagePlugin.ts`) also tints every card to the ground it stands on from a per-instance attribute the clutter rebuild writes. The grass floor is hex-tiled from the 2 m grass texture (`groundHexParams.ts`, `shaders/groundHex.fragment.fx`), gains a finer normal and a between-blades occlusion near the eye, carries a lush-to-dry macro tint the tufts share, and inside the blade field's reach is pulled toward a shaded thatch by the ground cover each terrain vertex carries. The trail is a footpath sunk into the sim's ground (`sim/trail.ts`), painted per fragment from a two-row segment table as four bands that wander with the lattice noise, wear along their length and go wet with the weather (`trailPaint.ts`, `trailBenchParams.ts`), reading as neglected: leaf and needle drifts painted where the ground cover's own duff lies thick, gravel washed out to bare dirt in patches of the bed's own noise, litter along its margin, and the grass beside it only lightly trampled at rebuild. Inside 18 m on the high and medium tiers the near field is opaque blade clumps built in code, placed independently of the cards: `bladeField.ts` walks a half-metre lattice and asks the sim's own grass gate how much grass belongs at each cell, so blades stand wherever the floor is grass rather than only where a meadow card happens to sit, and a cell's strength thins the clump continuously instead of flipping it on or off. The meadow's near cards stand under them as the cover, with the blades as the detail on top, dithering in from a metre out so none stands as a flat plane at the feet. Four characters (fine grass, tussock, broad-leaf weed and a flower-bearing one) cross three distance tiers and three clump sizes into the thirty-six buckets of `bladeMeshes.ts`, one thin-instance draw each on three tier materials, with the coarsest tier reaching the cards' own 18 m seam. Babylon draws a thin-instanced mesh whole or not at all, and these buckets surround the eye, so the blade buckets and the grass class's card buckets keep their full instance lists on the CPU and draw only the prefix inside a frustum widened by 6° and pushed back a metre, turned with the camera's roll, cut again after a rebuild or once the camera has turned 3.5°, rolled a degree or moved half a metre (`grassCull.ts`), with only that prefix uploaded and the draw count unchanged; the low tier draws its grass cards whole. `bladeClump.ts` builds each character as strips of blades; a four-edge collapse in the vertex shader grows a blade in from its tier's inner edge and shrinks it back to its root at the outer one, so neighbouring tiers hand over without a visible seam. The first pass of the post chain is multisampled (`MSAA_SAMPLES`) on those tiers; the low tier draws no blades. The near field reads one ground-cover field in the sim (`groundCover`, `sim/clutter.ts`) that thins grass toward every non-grass neighbour and thickens it inside, and where it thins a second lattice (`duffField.ts`, `duffMeshes.ts`, `duffClump.ts`) lays code-built dead leaves, twigs and branches on the floor, whose paint follows the same field; a cell buys the clump size its cover earns, dithered so no contour forms. The low tier gets the paint only. The understory is two more of the sim's clutter classes, placed by habitat (`sim/clutter.ts`): sword fern, the forest's own fern model in clumps a metre or two across, in patches under the canopy on low ground, a trace in the open and thinning uphill; and an evergreen shrub layer in thickets, as salal behind the beach and on drained slopes under the lowland canopy and as huckleberry and heath from the high forest to the treeline. The shrub has no model: its mound of oval leaves on arching stems is built in code (`shrubClump.ts`), two characters at two levels of detail, with its reflection all but taken away, since a mound of glossy facets mirrors the sky and reads grey. A fallen log is seated by its trunk and not its bounding box (`logSeat.ts`): a line fitted through the model's underside, robust to the root flare under it, is laid on a line fitted through the ground at five places along the log, and the log is sunk a fifth of its diameter into the floor. A log is 9 to 17 m long and over a metre through, and the sim lets none lie across a trail or the road: a tree that would fall across either stands dead instead (`logCrossesPath`, `sim/vegetation.ts`). Its upper side is tinted to moss by vertex colours laid over the bark, and seven logs in ten are nurse logs, each with a few ferns and conifer seedlings along its top, placed from the log's own hash on the log's own matrix (`nurseLog.ts`); the seedling is built in code. The forest plants nothing of its own at a tree's foot: the fern and the shrub it once stood there, one a tree and scaled with it, are retired for the two clutter classes. Wet ground has its own class: skunk cabbage and devil's club, both built in code (`wetPlantClump.ts`), along seeps (winding ribbons, the middle band of a low-frequency noise, under the lowland canopy on ground no steeper than a bench) and in a ring round a lake outside its bare shore. The high ground has its own too: beargrass (most of it tussocks, some under a tall flowering stalk), lupine and mountain heather, built in code (`highPlantClump.ts`), in drifts from the high forest up to the snow and mostly in the open. The one broadleaf tree is an alder, a third species of the regeneration cohort with its own model: it stands where `alderHabitat` (`sim/vegetation.ts`) says the ground is its own, along the road's verge, round a lake outside its tree margin and along the seeps (`sim/seep.ts`, the field the wet-ground plants read too), on lowland ground; there most cells hold a tree whatever the forest's own density is, and most of those trees are alders. Along the top of the beach lies a drift line, a clutter class of the same trunk bleached and laid along the shore, seated the same way. Rocks and boulders are cut into fractured stone at load (`rockRelief.ts`) — seeded planes, flat facets, a per-facet tone — four ways per model, with instances spread across the cuts by hash. The cut only ever removes material, so it stays inside the collision boxes the simulation declares and changes nothing in `sim/`; where a plane cleaves a boulder's top the box, sized from the uncut height, is left standing up to 0.30 m above the visible stone (`docs/rendering/2026-09-23-rock-relief-design.md`, §3). Rock ground too steep to stand on is dressed with two instanced rock-wall models. Where they stand is decided in the simulation (`sim/cliffField.ts`), because a wall is part of the world every peer must agree on, so its constants are in the level id: on a 12 m lattice, one cell in ten whose jittered point is steep rock lays a run of modules along the contour, each module wherever its centre and the ground under its whole above-ground body — the corners of that box and the edge midpoints of its longer axes, the top edge among them — are all rock and below the simulation's stand limit by a margin, so a module never stands where a player can, facing downslope, leaning toward the ground normal but by no more than 20°, so it stands against the hillside instead of lying back along it, and sunk a third of its height. The renderer (`cliffField.ts`, `cliffMeshes.ts`) draws them in three LOD rings out to 400 m on the high tier, each tinted halfway toward the ground under it (`cliffTintPlugin.ts`). Each module is solid: a chunk pass (`sim/passes/cliffs.ts`) lays a row of axis-aligned boxes along it, one for each piece of at most 4 m of its length, that together contain the whole drawn module, the part the sink buries included, each with its uphill face moved back into the hill until the hillside reaches its top (so a hiker sliding down behind a wall lands on it rather than being caught against it), so a wall stops a hiker and blocks sight the way a boulder does.

The water is one `PBRMaterial` on a body's row (`mat_water_sea`, and `mat_water_lake_<i>` on the row a lake's murk gives, `lakeWaterRow`) with a plugin (`client/src/game/waterPlugin.ts`, `shaders/water*.fx`) that reads the bed's depth per pixel from a camera-local height texture baked from the sim's `elevationAt` a row a frame into a spare grid and swapped in whole (`bedHeight.ts`), discards on land so the mesh's vertices on land draw no pixel, and transmits the bed by the attenuation measured for each body (`waterShading.ts`: Kd per channel, the deep-water colour, and the shelter that scales Cox and Munk's wind roughness). It holds the reflected ray above the horizon, and on the high tier (WebGPU only) writes unblended in a later rendering group after copying the opaque pass and reading its resolved depth (`waterFrame.ts`), so what stands in the water is attenuated by its own depth. A second plugin (`wetPlugin.ts`) darkens and glosses the ground, the props and the characters below a body's wet line, inside that body's footprint. A murky lake carries a skin of duckweed and algae in the same plugin, laid from its depth, its murk and a seeded noise, and its reeds, cattails and pond-lilies are built in code and drawn by `waterPlants.ts` from the sim's clutter classes. The sea moves (`client/src/game/ocean*.ts`, `shaders/ocean*.fx`). A swell seeded per world, inside the real coast's range of height, period and direction, is a sum of twelve components (eight on the low tier) in pairs close in frequency, so it arrives in sets; each component's phase is integrated along the coast from tables built at load from the bed's own profiles (`oceanTables.ts`), blending from the bay's to the cove's over 250 m past the cove's ends, so a wave slows, steepens and turns toward the beach as the bed rises, and a rule by the depth under it (Weggel's breaker index) caps it and spills it into a bore and its white water across the cove's bed (`oceanWaves.ts`), sheltered behind the headlands. The tables, the components and the coastline reach the GPU as one float texture, which the water plugin reads in the vertex stage, displacing seven stitched rings from 1 m and handing the swell's normal, height and envelope on, and in the fragment stage, for the break and the foam from that envelope. A wind sea from the weather and the hour, following the wind a minute behind and, under an offshore wind, held near the shore to the Coastal Engineering Manual's fetch law, rides on the swell, with whitecaps by Callaghan's coverage: a GPU FFT of three cascades in WebGPU compute on the high tier (`oceanGpuFft.ts`), a 20 s loop baked in a worker at load on the medium tier (`oceanLoopBake.ts`), the bump's normals on the low tier. The sea runs on the sim's tick (`sharedSeconds`, `oceanWindSea.ts`), so every player sees the same crest at the same moment, and `crestAt` and `boreArrivals` give the shore's later work each wave's height, period and phase.

The lake has its own life (`client/src/game/waterLife.ts`), render-side like the animals: where it lives is placed once from the world's seed and its lake (`waterLifeField.ts`), the midges' swarm markers over the shore's shrubs, reeds, driftlogs and snags and over open water, the darners' beats along the rim, the skimmers' perches, the damselflies' stems in the beds of reeds and wet plants and the chorus frogs' voices at the water's edge; a perch on land stands on its own ground, and only a reed's perch or a stem over water stands on the water. What is there follows the hour, the weather, the wind and the dread (`waterLifeParams.ts`), each share eased over 3 s: the midges from 05:15 to 07:00 and across dusk, up from 17:45, full from 18:15 to 19:15 and gone by a quarter to eight, out of a strong wind and the rain; the dragonflies by day in sun, the darners flying on into dusk at a third of their share until 19:00; in a strong wind the darners take cover while the skimmers and the damselflies sit perched; under a cloud only the skimmers, on their perches; the frogs from dusk until before dawn; and none of it by the middle of the dread. The midges are one thin-instanced draw of camera-facing specks on one shader material (`midgeSwarms.ts`, `shaders/midge.*.fx`) with no texture read: each midge's place about its swarm's centre is three sinusoids an axis from its own seed and the shared seconds (`midgeMotion.ts`), so the swarms move alike on every player's screen and no per-midge data is uploaded a frame, only a few uniforms and a table of 32 rows, each row a swarm's centre after the wind's shift and surge, its size, its count and its presence. The rows are the markers and one over each player who has stood still in the shore band for 10 s, and the tier's midges in view (800, 2,000 and 4,000) are shared among them by size and distance, a far swarm thinning to a tenth at 60 m and gone beyond 80 m; close to the eye a midge's coverage never falls below a floor, which fades out from 6 to 15 m, so a far swarm is faint specks. On the low tier, with no post chain, the midges' stage tones their light with the frame's exposure, tone map, encode and contrast, as the sky dome's does. The motes keep pollen and frost only. The dragonflies are a few dozen small state machines on the CPU (`dragonflyBehaviour.ts`), the darners beating along the rim, the skimmers sallying from their perches and back and the damselflies hopping from stem to stem in their bed, drawn as thin-instanced cards, one draw a kind, whose wings shimmer through the wing plugin (`dragonflies.ts`). Midges and dragonflies draw among the rain, the mist and the motes, in the water's group on the high tier. The sound goes through looping positional sources in the ambient graph (`ambientAudio.ts`): the nearest swarms' hum, synthesized at a pitch that follows the summer's temperature, the dragonflies' wings, and the frogs' calls, which fall silent around each player and near the Hollow and, once players have left a voice, are heard from it again only after its own 20 to 40 s (`frogChorus.ts`, `waterLifeAudio.ts`, `insectVoices.ts`). The calls are recordings, each voice its own single call at its own rate (the synthesized call while its clip is not in, or if it fails to load), over a far chorus of two loops across the water that thins while a player stands among the near frogs, falls quiet around a player as the voices do, and goes silent with the dread and the Hollow. All of it is mixed into the ambient graph's world bus, which reaches the master through the stare's low-pass and then a brick-wall limiter, so the frogs, a hum over the head and a rustle together cannot clip. With the camera out of reach of all of it, nothing is stepped.

The people are models from one character pool (`client/src/game/characterModel.ts`): every other player is one of five rangers, chosen by player id so every peer sees the same one, and the Hollow is `hollow.antlered`, drawn at twice a player's height over an unchanged hull, its clip following its measured speed and its eyes lit a saturated emissive red, the tell that reads from far off (`hollowLook.ts`). The things placed once and seen up close — the car and the board at the trailhead (`trailheadMeshes.ts`; the board's face is a plane painted when the match starts, `boardPaint.ts`: the trail's name routed into the wood, and a map of the world's own trails, the missing hiker's poster and a sheet of rules on paper), the body at the crest (`bodyMesh.ts`) and the fingerposts, one plank per place name painted on thin planes, each plank at a height of its own on a post stretched to hold them (`signMeshes.ts`) — are static models loaded then placed with only their `LOD0` root enabled (`staticModel.ts`). Each stands on the box the simulation collides with, and a model that has not loaded, or never will, leaves a stand-in drawn in its place: a capsule for a hiker or the Hollow, the sim's own boxes for the car, the board and a sign post, a timber cross for the body. No collider is ever invisible.

Rain is a camera-locked wrapped volume, not a particle emitter (`client/src/game/rain.ts`, `rainPlugin.ts`, `rainParams.ts`): one thin-instanced quad mesh holds a fixed population of streaks (3,000, 10,000 and 24,000 by tier), each a seed in the unit cube that a vertex-stage material plugin places by a fold of its seed inside a 24 by 20 by 24 m box biased 6 m along the view, so the box is full at every height on every frame, the field of drops stands still in the world while the box slides over it, and nothing is uploaded per frame; four fall-speed classes slant by the wind, the streak's length is the frame's motion blur from a smoothed frame time, its alpha fades toward the sky and brightens in the headlamp's cone, and the fog's density and greyness follow the rain (`weather.ts`). On the medium and high tiers a top-down height map (`rainMap.ts`, a 512-texel render target over 96 m around the player on the scene's custom render targets, re-rendered once each time the player has moved 8 m, drawing the two inner terrain rings, the props, the cliffs and the water through three cheap shader materials) stores each texel's top height, its transmission (open ground 1, canopy by the terrain's own density, props and cliffs 0) and a ceiling lift; the streaks read it and fade under cover, splash rings (`rainSplash.ts`) land on its height around the player, and a sparse vertical drip volume falls only under canopy, driven by a canopy-water value that fills a minute into the rain and drains over ten (`canopyWaterStep` in `weather.ts`), which the ambient audio's drip layer hears as well (`ambientAudio.ts`). Every PBR material given a porosity cap (the trees, the understory, the clutter, the cliffs, the props; the terrain keeps its own trail-paint wetness) darkens and glosses with the weather's wetness by a porosity rule (`wetPlugin.ts`), the trail's puddles carry four layers of rings hashed in the fragment behind a branch on the rain (`trailPaint.ts`), and on the tiers with the post chain a `lens` pass between FXAA and `finish` refracts the frame through a code-generated droplet field and a few sliding drops, frosting toward a four-tap blur of the scene, strongest looking up into the rain, cleared under the trees, and detached from the chain while idle (`lensParams.ts`, `shaders/lens.fragment.fx`). The measured costs are in [the rain system's verification](docs/rendering/2026-10-01-rain-system-verification.md).

## Assets

- `client/assets/models/*.glb`, `client/assets/textures/*.webp` and `client/assets/audio/*.mp3` are committed through Git LFS.
- `client/assets/catalog.json` has three lists: `assets` (models — `id`, `kind` of `character`, `creature`, `environment` or `prop`, `output`, and for animated models an `animations` map from the role the game asks for (`idle`, `walk`, `attack`, `death`, `graze`, `run`, `alert`) to the clip name in the file), `textures` (ground layers — `id`, `output`), and `audio` (wildlife calls — `id`, `output`).
- Assets are imported with Vite `?url` globs (`client/src/game/assetUrls.ts`), so production URLs are content-hashed and cached as immutable. A catalog `output` with no file behind it throws at startup rather than 404ing into a missing mesh.
- `CREDITS.md` credits third-party work; `client/src/game/credits.ts` parses it for the in-game Credits screen.

### Model conventions

What the game assumes of every model file:

- Units are metres; the origin is at the model's feet (or base), and forward is +Z.
- Models streamed by distance have `LOD0`, `LOD1` and `LOD2` root nodes, each holding that level's meshes; the game finds them by those names.
- Clip names are the ones the catalog's `animations` map gives.
- A part the game repaints is found by its material: the Hollow's eyes are its one material with a non-zero emissive factor. The trailhead board is placed by its numbers instead: the board's face 2 m by 1 m toward +Z, its centre 1.37 m above the board's foot and 0.159 m in front of its centre plane, which is where the game stands its own painted plane.
- Saturated red belongs to the Hollow's eyes alone (`HOLLOW_EYE_COLOR`, `client/src/game/hollowLook.ts`), so its tell never camouflages against the world. It is a convention for every model and material, not a check: no test enforces it.

## Hosting

The site is on Firebase Hosting at `games.csarko.sh/dayhike`; the signaling server is a single Cloud Run instance, because rooms live in its memory. Terraform in `_infra/` owns both, plus DNS. `tools/deploy/` builds and ships them and verifies production independently.

## Testing

Vitest runs three roots — `client`, `server`, `tools` — all headless. Babylon code is tested under `NullEngine`; `client/test/game/catalogModels.test.ts` loads every catalog model and checks its clips and LOD roots.
